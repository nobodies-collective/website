Friday 16 October

Welcome & Year in Review
⚡ All togetherFriday 16 October

What this session was

The opening session of ALM 2026 — a welcome, a practical briefing, and a warm-up to get everyone oriented before the working weekend began.

What happened

Welcome

Daniela welcomed attendees and introduced the org team: Doreen (venue), Yllza (meals & shifts), Yakir and Martian (equipment & tech), Céline and Daniela (programme), Cupcake (ALM finance and travel reimbursements), and Leaf/Frank supporting remotely during the weekend. Doreen ran through house rules for the Vidalia space.

Lessons Learned

Céline opened a short lessons-learned round, drawing on the Discord channel and inviting the room to share experiences from 2026. Stories ranged from “emergency mud baths” and lost Jeep keys to sound policy breaches by a certain Trojan horse; setting a candid, energetic tone for the weekend.

Annual Roadmap framework

Daniela then presented the proposed Annual Roadmap framework: a film-industry-inspired production stage model (Development → Pre-production → On-site → Post-event) with hard milestones: event dates confirmed and budget voted by December, tickets on sale in January, barrio placement moving earlier in the year. This generated good discussion, particularly around how early barrio leads could realistically engage and how earlier ticket sales would help cash flow.

Finance Overview

Peter gave a Finance Overview of the 2026 cycle: ~5,000 transactions, a 20% margin in the first year as a new entity, and a cash flow crunch caused by Stripe holding a significant portion of revenue until after the event. A new automated finance system (expense reports → approval → accounting → bank) is now in place for 2027. The Finance Working Group kicks off the following Monday, open to anyone who wants a say in the budget.

Asset Acquisition Working Group

Ellen briefed the room on the Asset Acquisition Working Group: NCA leased the containers for the 2026 event; the goal now is to separate the event timeline from the acquisition process. Next steps would be putting in place a lease for the material assets for the 2027 event, and to complete a dossier for GNSL as the first step toward formal acquisition. All communications go through the working group; single point of contact with GNSL is Ben Ford.

Facilitation Guidelines briefing

Cupcake and Ellen closed with a Facilitation Guidelines briefing for the weekend’s sessions: roles (facilitator, secretary/timekeeper, hybrid guardian), five principles (autonomy, speak with intention, one point per person, speak for yourself, be for not against), hand signals for speaking order, clarifications, disagreement, and temperature checks, plus a consensus model (agree / agree with reservations / block) rather than majority voting.

Outcomes / decisions

  • Annual Roadmap stage framework proposed; turned into a Gantt chart post-ALM
  • Finance Working Group confirmed, starting Monday 19 October, open to all
  • Asset Acquisition Working Group update shared; next step is GNSL dossier
  • Facilitation framework distributed to all session leads for the weekend
Working Group Speed Dating
⚡ All togetherFriday 16 October

What this session was

A rapid-fire pitch round for all the weekend’s working sessions. Each person got a couple of minutes to explain what their session was about and what they needed from participants, so people could make an informed choice about where to spend their time.

Sessions pitched

Event-Time Ops & Org Hub

Working group: Event-Time Ops

A proposal to design a dedicated physical hub for event-time operations, somewhere people can come with questions, needs, and emergencies, rather than routing everything through Malfare or calling coordinators directly. The session would map what that hub looks like, what it covers, and how handovers and shifts work from it.

Beyond Safehaus: What Should Crew Rest Look Like?

Working group: Safehaus

A rethink of how the organisation provides rest for its crew across all phases: setup, event, and strike. This year’s AC cabins reduced the need for some volunteers to leave site; the traditional Safehaus inadequacy brought questions about what we need from that space; and the strike decompression rotation shone a light on a possibility for a different resting need post-event. The session would explore formalising that model: dedicated sleeping configurations in cabins, bunk setups, and making the decompression space an official, rotational part of the strike plan.

Clearing the Air on Sound

Working group: Sound Policy

The sound policy needs an update as it touches almost every area of the event: power, malfare, barrio support, creativity, art cars. The session would work through a structured list of outstanding questions: barrio sound rules, art piece and art car policy, year-on-year continuity of enforcement, and how to support nomads navigating sound complaints. Goal: a solid enough discussion to produce a formal proposal before city planning kicks off barrio placement.

Designing Our Site: Org Footprint, Structures and Spaces

Working group: Site Design

A proposal to collectively design the site, starting with the organisation’s structures; doing now in the development phase, rather than after barrio placement during the pre-production phase. This year, things like the Malfare sleeping cabins ending up next to Werkhaus speakers only became obvious once everything was built. The session would gather department-by-department spatial and structural needs so city planning, infrastructure, and power can design the city around the org, not the other way around.

MoE/MoN: What Are We Building?

Working group: MoE/MoN

MoN has ambitions to be the beating heart of the event, but the equipment is outdated and the vision needs refreshing. The session would explore what it would take — new kit, new concept — to make that a reality.

How We’re Structured: Roles, Responsibilities & Reporting

Working group: Organisational Structure

A structured session based on the survey sent to leads, coordinators, and board members. Topics: department definitions, lead and coordinator roles, board roles, selection and deselection processes, and payroll transparency. The aim is to come out with formal definitions and agreed processes, not just a discussion.

Policy around Politics: Working Group proposal

A first conversation about how the organisation handles political questions: pro/contra positions from attendees, pronoun signage objections, geopolitical tensions on site. No solutions are expected in this slot; the goal is to start a working group that can think of ways to give leads in roles that handle these situations —mainly the Participant Wellness teams— clear definitions of their role’s scope and guardrails for how to deal with it.

Werkhaus: From Volunteer Camp to Org Infrastructure

Working group: Werkhaus

Werkhaus sits in a blurry space between the organisation and a barrio, which caused problems this year: fluff volunteer confusion, ice budget disputes, and almost no one stepping up to run the barrio until the last minute. The session would try to draw clearer lines: what’s the org’s responsibility, what’s the barrio’s, and how do we make sure it’s properly staffed in advance.

New Site: Purchase and Infrastructure

Working group: El Sitio

A department-by-department input session on what a site move would actually mean: costs of projects, potential savings, and practical considerations from the people on the ground. The current assumption is renting the same site for June 2027; but if we were to try the New Site, the decision needs to be made by December. This session feeds the business case.

How Sunday closes

Each session lead will give a brief outcome update in the closing plenary on Sunday morning. Attendees are asked to check out with the venue team before leaving. The ALM feedback survey goes out Monday around noon.

Saturday 17 October

Event-Time Ops & Org Hub
⚡ All togetherSaturday 17 October

What this session was

A working group to design a dedicated Event-Time Operations Hub for 2027: a physical space and role that takes over the shift check-in, check-out, and handover functions that currently fall by default on Malfare, Production, and Onsite Coordinators.

The problem it’s solving

Right now, when volunteers show up to start or finish a shift, there’s no clear place to go or a clear person to receive them. They end up at Werkhaus, Malfare, or Production depending on who they find first. Malfare in particular absorbs a huge amount of this. Radio handovers happening in the same space where sensitive conversations are taking place. Coordinators get pulled in constantly for things that aren’t strictly their job. The goal is to give this its own home and role.

What was agreed

Location

The hub will sit within the existing Welfare Compound. It will share a front-of-house space with Malfare (keeping the visible, welcoming face people already know) but have a separate office for operational work, so Malfare’s private and sensitive space stays protected. Being inside the compound also means the role isn’t isolated at night and can draw on the collective knowledge of the people working nearby. Signage across the whole compound needs a significant upgrade: big, visible, lit at night, with clear directional arrows to Event Ops Hub, Welfare, Malfare, Mission Control, and Safer Space.

Physical setup

An AC cabin (required for radio storage) with a shaded outdoor area. A lockbox system for after-hours access to kits, as used by Kunsthaus and in the production offices this year. A freezer and shelf for volunteer fluff.

What the hub manages

Check-in and check-out for all event-time volunteer shifts, with a printed checklist for each role. Distribution of radios, kits, vouchers, fluff boxes and shift binders. Tracking who showed up, who was late, and flagging gaps. A charging station for radios, with spare day-to-day radios on hand; main radio stock and batteries remain in the Radio cabin.

The role

The existing Radio Ranger role will be repurposed and expanded into this Event Ops role, taken out of Radios entirely so the radio team can focus on the radios. Ideally staffed by two people given the volume and spread of handovers. They carry a radio and are on call between handover windows. Radio Rangers’ current responsibilities for giving nomads their bags, vouchers, and binders fold naturally into this.

Shift standardisation

Full standardisation isn’t realistic given the range of departments, but shifts can be staggered so handovers are spread across a window rather than all hitting at once. Some flexibility is essential: Safer Space runs two-hour shifts for accessibility reasons; departments need to be able to hold nomads over during incidents. The rough overnight handover picture from Humans: 11pm welfare lead, midnight welfare, 2am nomads and interpreters, 4am welfare, 7am welfare shift lead, 8am nomads and power. Post-ALM action: map all event-time shifts in full to design the hub’s actual operating rhythm. Event Ops shifts would need to start between shifts, not along with others, to allow some time for landing.

Scope boundary

This role manages shifts, not the jobs within them. They are not following the water truck or doing welfare briefings. Departmental shift leads still brief their own people; this role handles the logistics wrapper around that.

What’s still open

  • Full shift map across all event-time departments (post-ALM)
  • Whether the role needs to be formally 24/7 or can operate on a staffed-plus-on-call model
  • Fallback coverage if the role no-shows (radio team as backstop was suggested)
  • Humans feature request: ability to mark volunteer no-shows in real time, and an on-call notification system so available people can be pinged when a gap appears mid-event

Next step

Daniela to write up a document proposal for circulation and comment. Physical placement to be worked through in the Site Design session.

Beyond Safehaus: What Should Crew Rest Look Like?
ParallelSaturday 17 October

What this session was

A working group to redesign how the organisation provides rest for its setup, event, and strike crew — moving away from the unreliable, badly-located safehaus model toward something more intentional, more self-managed, and actually restorative.

The problem

The current safehaus setup has never worked well. The vendor is unreliable and volatile — this year, two houses were confirmed and only one materialised on arrival, and even that one was poor: noisy (next to a supermarket with 5am deliveries), cramped, and not genuinely restful. Transportation to and from site creates its own friction — people who left relaxed came back more stressed. The roles managing it have bounced between volunteer coordination and production with neither fully owning it, and nobody wanting to repeat the job. Overcrowding and double-booking happened again this year because different coordinators were managing beds without a shared system.

On the upside: AC cabins on site this year made a real difference. People slept better, siestas actually happened, and the general energy on site was noticeably better than previous years. The strike decompression rotation — people leaving, resting properly somewhere else, coming back fresh — also worked well.

What was agreed

The group landed on a hybrid model built around four components:

On-site sleeping cabins

Two dedicated rest cabins, each with two bunk beds (four people per cabin, eight total). AC is required — which means the power load needs to be planned from the start, not improvised. A phased cabin power-up plan should be built alongside crew arrival schedules so consumption is managed. A shade structure over the cabins was floated as a way to reduce the cooling load. These cabins need clear rules, a booking/allocation system, and a lighting signal (red = do not disturb) to make the “don’t bother people who are resting” norm actually stick.

Hotel rooms in town

Eight allocated rooms (plus one or two held back as emergency buffer) at a hotel in Sariñena or nearby, for people who need genuine off-site separation — a private room, a proper shower, a door that shuts. The hotel should ideally offer a laundry service so people aren’t making separate trips. No communal safehaus space is needed: once Werkhaus is up early (as it was this year), on-site social space exists and people stopped wanting to leave.

A bus route

The biggest unlock. A regular minibus run — probably three times a day, stopping at the org area, gate, the pool, town, and the train station — removes the current dependency on volunteer drivers and on coordinators having to manage ad hoc lifts. Daniela is pursuing this with the comarca president, who has indicated the municipality has a minibus available for this kind of use. If this happens, people become self-reliant for transport and the management burden drops significantly.

Strike decompression

The same hotel logic applied to strike: a block of allocated spots at the decompression site Ben Ford has a relationship with (~1 hour from site), rotated across strike volunteers so everyone gets a couple of days off. Followed by a shared decompression for the core strike team at the end — one or two days together before everyone disperses.

What’s still open

Budget

All of this will cost more than the current safehaus (~€2k). Daniela to cost it up and bring it to the Finance Working Group as a line item. The argument: well-rested volunteers stay longer, perform better, and come back next year.

Allocation system

The rough formula is one rest day per week on site, pre-allocated and visible in Humans, with the option to self-manage trades. Pre-event and first-crew blocks of volunteers should get their rest days mapped before they arrive, not ad hoc on site. Nurz flagged that some people will need emergency headroom beyond their allocation — illness, intense job blocks — so the system needs buffer built in.

Who owns the admin

This has historically fallen between volunteer coordination and production. It needs a clear owner, and the goal is to make Humans do the heavy lifting so neither department is manually managing beds.

Local vendor relationships

Laundry access, local sourcing, and the post office/reception space concept (using the former “unsafehaus” premises as an org base in town for the pre-event period) are adjacent ideas to explore.

Power planning for cabins

Needs to be integrated into the phased site power-up schedule.

Designing Our Site: Org Footprint, Structures and Spaces
ParallelSaturday 17 October

What this session was

A working group to begin collective, intentional site design for 2027; gathering department-by-department spatial and infrastructure needs early in the development phase, so city planning, infrastructure, and power can design the city around the organisation rather than retrofitting it afterwards.

Why now

This year, site layout decisions got made too late or by default. Malfare’s sleeping cabins ended up next to Werkhaus speakers. Generators blocked the front of Malfare from the playa. Barrier Support was too central during setup and left an ugly gap once the event started. The proposal is to fix the org footprint first, then build the barrio map around it instead of the other way round.

Department needs gathered

Production

Needs to be adjacent to the vendors’ road: first stop for trucks, deliveries, and authorities, without routing them through the event. Requires open space for vehicle access and parking, an AC office for computer work, and a meeting room for legal or authority visits. Open to sharing office space with other departments rather than maintaining separate cabins. Floated the idea of a shared “production village” compound with hot-desking, printing, and supply storage for multiple departments.

Power

Generators need to be placed as close to equidistant from consumption points as possible, fenced (flammable), and accessible by road for diesel trucks and forklifts. Miss Piggy needs a dedicated fenced prep area with a worktable, shade, and tool storage for building distro boxes. The solar gate area has the same road access and cabin requirements. Power currently shares cabin space with production for printing and plan updates, happy to continue that. UPS units for Red Cross comms flagged as equipment need (not placement-critical).

Radio

Moving toward the Event Ops hub model, so no longer needs to be directly accessible from the playa. Needs an AC cabin space (lockable), close to the hub and Malfare. Happy to share the cabin with Geeks or finance; just needs desk space and a section for radio charging and day-to-day kit. The bulk of batteries and equipment stays in the Geeks’ cabin.

Barrier Support

Needs road access at the back for heavy equipment pickup (pallets, water cubes). Storage should be in a lockable container or taped-off area: equipment was being freely helped-to this year. Wants to be placed towards the back/road side rather than mid-playa, so the area doesn’t create an eyesore during the event. Needs a shaded outdoor area with seating for barrio registration groups, plus an AC cabin with internet and power for charging Makita batteries. Happy to share internet and printer access with production if co-located. Ellen has a sketch showing how the compound could back onto the road.

Malfare / Welfare

Needs much more frontage: this year Ohana and a generator were in front of it, limiting visibility. Wants a wider open face toward the playa with clear signage and lighting. AC cabin for leads’ office with secure storage for incident reports and confidential documents, plus AC sleep cabins nearby. Closer proximity to Red Cross (shared toilet access), and the Event-Time Ops hub (for fluff volunteers). Safer Space has its own container and needs to be near, but not necessarily front-and-centre. The year-before-last’s layout between Malfare and Werkhaus was flagged as a good reference to go back to.

Werkhaus Camping

Needs quiet in its sleeping area. The LNT container (Kermit) at the corner was waking campers with morning discussions. Kermit should move: behind Container Village, between Container Village and Cantina or to Production Village, were suggested as better spots. Container orientation should be rotated 90° for better shade; this has been discussed before but didn’t make it onto site. Shower drainage also needs to be factored in.

Werkhaus Barrio

Possibly changing structure design next year (container vs rigging; still TBC with infra team). Needs clear playa frontage as a barrio, not buried in site infrastructure. Must be able to play music without disrupting Malfare. Sound zone: far end of Yellow. Proximity to Cantina important given the volume of movement between the two during setup and event. Internet access a nice-to-have for coordinators.

LNT (Kermit)

Happy to move away from Werkhaus Camping. Needs to be near production and Toolhaus. Wants to be on the map so people can find it. Road access needed occasionally. No shower directly above it.

No Info / Ice Ice Baby

Currently well-placed relative to production and Werkhaus; the proximity of this year works. Needs a visibility mechanism (flag or light) so people can see from the playa whether ice is available. More shade for volunteers. Rain protection over freezers. Power for freezers and cashless terminal. Wi-Fi for cashless terminal. Fixed, un-stealable seating for volunteers. Lockable AC cabin (shared with Barrier Support in event phase is viable). Phone charging point for participants. Desk space and locker inside.

Toilet design — Sanitation session (Shitshow)

A presentation on the vulva urinal trials in 2026. Two types were tested: squatting toilets (Lapees) and standing urinals (informally called ChatChichiPees). A post-event survey found both had advocates and detractors, with preferences varying by anatomy, height, and personal comfort. Key feedback: squatting units can spray; standing units need to be lower and wider; fencing felt intimidating especially at night; signage was unclear for many users.

Decisions: both types to continue in 2027, aiming for equal numbers of penis and vulva urinals. Improvements needed: lower and varied heights for the standing units, better splash design (angled pipe section suggested), friendlier enclosure materials (fabric over fencing), clearer signage, and a dedicated team that includes people who use them. Placement needs excavator involvement to pre-dig holes before site setup begins, which means it has to be planned early/exactly to avoid re-excavation. The sanitation company had no issues with the service access this year.

Structural decisions — open questions

The session closed with two discussion points that will need follow-up:

How do we decide to build something or not?

A prioritised list of must-haves is needed, with a calendar trigger — if a project isn’t confirmed by X date, it doesn’t get built. Everyone’s project feels essential, which makes ranking hard.

How do we separate working cabins from sleeping cabins?

Clear designation matters — people walking into a working cabin to find a stranger napping in it (or vice versa) creates friction and, in some cases, security risks for stored equipment. The proposal is explicit phase-based allocation: a cabin can be a rest cabin in week one, transition to an office as departments arrive, and potentially revert during strike. Shared AC space should be the default wherever security needs allow.

Accessibility and changing crew demographics were raised as a broader consideration — the organisation’s long-term volunteers are getting older, and site design should start actively accounting for mobility and health needs.

Next steps

  • Daniela to circulate a post-session survey to departments not represented at the session.
  • A follow-up call in a few weeks to consolidate the full needs list and begin mapping it into a city design document.
