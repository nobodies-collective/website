Event Dates 2027: what we know so far

The General Assembly will be asked on 17 September 2026 whether to move the event out of its established early-July slot. This page collects the material that exists today, while it is still incomplete.

Last updated: 6 August 2026 Decision: General Assembly, 17 September 2026 Status: Work in progress

This is not an information pack. It is a snapshot of what has been gathered so far, published early for people who would rather see the raw state of the work than wait for it to be finished. Parts of it are preliminary desk research that has not yet been verified. Parts of it will change. Some of it may turn out to be wrong.

Nothing on this page should be read as a settled position of the board, of the association, or of anyone in it.

A proper information pack is being prepared. Ahead of the vote we aim to produce a short, readable booklet — one clear handout for the decision, setting out the benefits and drawbacks of each candidate date, in plain language and without needing hours of background reading.

It is intended to include feedback from other burns across Europe on how a move would affect them and their attendees, polling of the people who actually come to the event, and a more rigorous version of the fire-risk and environmental material summarised below.

That booklet will be circulated with enough time before the 17 September vote for members to read it and decide. If you would rather wait for it, that is entirely reasonable — this page exists for those who want to dig deeper before it is ready.

1. What is actually being decided

The 2026 event ran 7–12 July. Without a decision to the contrary, 2027 would sit in the equivalent early-July window — the working assumption in the planning material is 5–11 July 2027.

The question going to the General Assembly is whether to keep that slot or move to a different window. It is a members’ decision, not a board decision. The board’s job here is to put a well-prepared question in front of the membership, with the best information it can assemble in the time available.

Who decides

The General Assembly — the members of the association, each with one vote.

When

Thursday 17 September 2026, 20:00 CEST, online. Moved from 3 September to allow more preparation time.

Agenda fixed

2 September 2026. The event-dates question is on it.

Likely mechanism

Ranked voting with an option to reject all choices — under discussion, since no single window may win outright.

“No change” is a real option on the ballot. The board resolved to hold the vote rather than let the date be settled by inaction; if the information turns out not to be good enough, members can vote not to move.

The board has taken no position on which dates to choose. Views among board members genuinely differ — on whether to move at all, and on whether September is too soon to decide well. Those exchanges are in the published minutes rather than summarised here, so you can read them first-hand.

Advance voting is expected to open a few days before the assembly, so members who cannot attend on the night can still vote. Only members of the association vote; applications approved after the assembly is called still count. Wider community polls will inform the vote without being the vote.

2. Timeline

  • 11 March 2026 Decreto 22/2026 approved in Aragón; published in the BOA (no. 53) on 18 March, three and a half months before the 2026 event.
  • 17 June 2026 Meeting with the Comarca de Los Monegros. The comarca reads the new automatic-suspension clause aloud and sets out its evacuation planning.
  • Mid-June 2026 Build begins. A fire roughly an hour away burns toward the site; ash falls on the build. It is brought under control. Evacuation plans — buses, receiving villages, Guardia Civil, Cruz Roja — were by this point genuinely in place, not hypothetical.
  • 26–28 June 2026 Roja Plus declared — three days, during build.
  • 7–12 July 2026 The event runs. The permit holds, nobody is hurt, there is no evacuation. Roja Plus is declared again during the event itself, bringing a legally binding lockdown on movement, gas cooking and open flame for part of one day. Region-wide red alert follows on 24 and 30 July.
  • 30 July 2026 Community call. The dates question is announced publicly, alongside the note that “the authorities have also strongly recommended scheduling outside the high-fire-risk period, as operational restrictions grow stricter every year.” Minutes.
  • 3 August 2026 Board votes to move the General Assembly from 3 to 17 September, specifically to allow more time to prepare the dates decision. Minutes.
  • 4 August 2026 Governance working group agrees to prepare a Swiss-style voting booklet for the decision. Minutes.
  • 5 August 2026 The candidate-window document goes to the regional authority for assessment. A response is expected around late September or October — i.e. after the vote.
  • 2 September 2026 GA agenda fixed.
  • 17 September 2026 General Assembly. Members vote.

One consequence of that sequence is worth stating plainly: the authority’s formal yes/no on each candidate window will most likely not be back in time. Whatever members vote on in September will be a decision made under real uncertainty, and the pack should say so rather than pretend otherwise.

3. Why a change of dates is on the table

The reason is not that July is uncomfortable. It is that a specific legal mechanism now exists which can void an authorisation the association already holds, and that mechanism is live for most of the year. What follows is the formal and regulatory picture as we currently understand it.

3.1 An automatic suspension written into law

Decreto 22/2026, de 11 de marzo (BOA no. 53, 18 March 2026). Article 4(h) provides that under a Roja Plus alert, public events in a forest environment are suspended, along with any event that could generate conditions favouring the start of a forest fire — and that authorisations already granted are suspended (“quedando suspendidas las autorizaciones concedidas”).

This is not a negotiation on the day. On the reading given to us by the comarca, the permit for the camping area ceases to be valid by operation of law the moment Roja Plus is declared. The clause is new in 2026.

A second clause is easy to overlook and matters just as much: Article 4(i) restricts motor-vehicle access to montes to the landowner and emergency or agricultural traffic. That alone would strand build, ingress and egress, independently of whether the event itself remained permitted.

3.2 The trigger is the alert level, not conditions on site

The regime applies because the site is monte de utilidad pública — public-utility forest land — within 400 m of forest mass. Nothing in it turns on measured air quality, on wind on the day, or on how well-run the event is. It fires on the published alert level for the area.

This is why operational excellence does not solve the problem. The event can be, and has been, held up locally as an example of good practice, and still be caught by a trigger that does not look at any of that.

3.3 The mechanism has already been used, on short notice

Roja Plus was declared for the first time on 16–18 August 2025. A festival at La Gabarda park was in progress. In the comarca’s words: “Tuvo que evacuar y punto” — it had to evacuate, full stop.

The warning window was roughly 8.5 hours: declared mid-afternoon, effective 23:59 the same day. A crowd of 2,000–2,400 people cannot be moved off a desert site in that time. That is why, for Elsewhere 2026, the authorities pre-planned an evacuation: buses, Guardia Civil involvement at senior rank, Cruz Roja, and villages designated to receive people. That work was done in advance of an event that then ran without needing it — which is the point. It is contingency planning only carried out by people who expect to have to use it.

3.4 Shelter-in-place has been proposed and refused

The argument that confinement is objectively safer than evacuation — 2,000 people in private vehicles crossing bone-dry terrain at 40°C creates more fire risk than staying put — was made to the authorities at length. The comarca agreed with it, and said so explicitly.

But the comarca’s position is not the binding one. The decision sits with the Gobierno de Aragón / Medio Natural, and the provincial director’s reported answer was “muy clara: que no” — if Roja Plus comes, you evacuate, “con la ley en la mano”, citing generators, fuel on site, kitchens, lighting and 2,000 people.

The significant part was the direction the warning came from: the local authorities most sympathetic to the event were the ones telling us they expected to be overruled.

3.5 The exposure window is six and a half months, not one

Aragón’s época de peligro — the fire-danger season during which Roja Plus can be declared at all — runs 1 April to 15 October (Orden of 20 February 2015, extended by Orden AGM/112/2021). Daily alert levels are published from 1 June to 15 October.

Declarations on record: 16–18 August 2025; 26–28 June 2026; and again during the 2026 event week in July — plus region-wide red alert on 24 and 30 July 2026.

So any date from April to mid-October carries the same structural exposure — September at lower probability than July, but identical in kind. This is the single most consequential point for anyone weighing options, and it cuts against several intuitive alternatives.

3.6 Frequency is rising, and the authorities say so explicitly

Figures given by the comarca in June 2026: an average of two fires a day over the preceding twenty days in Los Monegros alone; six fires in Huesca province on a single Sunday. Their summary was that conditions in mid-June are now what 15 July used to look like, and that measures will keep tightening year on year.

3.7 Permitting lead times force the decision now

Three permissions are needed, in sequence, and only the last of them is the event permit itself:

  1. INAGA — Aragón’s environmental permitting body. Assesses protected species, nesting periods and whether proposed dates are compatible with the site. A three-month statutory clock, but in practice anywhere from three months to a year; an unfavourable report is binding.
  2. CHE — the Ebro water authority. Watercourses, crossings, flood risk. A six-month clock, so if it is filed second it becomes the critical path.
  3. Comarca de Los Monegros — the event permit, which can only be applied for once the first two are in hand.

This is why the decision cannot be deferred until circumstances force a reaction: by the time you need to react, the paperwork window has already closed. It is also why the answers are unlikely to be back before 17 September.

A cautionary precedent from the same body: a 19,000-person festival at the embalse de La Sotonera was declared no procedente by INAGA in April 2026 — not on a substantive refusal, but because a full assessment could not be concluded before the proposed August dates. The calendar stopped the event, not the merits.

3.8 What we are not claiming

For the sake of a fair record, three things this material does not establish:

  • No authority has issued a formal order to move the dates. What exists is a legal mechanism, a demonstrated willingness to use it, and a strong recommendation.
  • There is no published prohibition on holding events in any particular month, for fire or for wildlife reasons.
  • The event was not shut down in 2026. It ran, through two Roja Plus periods, on the strength of a very large amount of largely invisible contingency work.

The honest framing is therefore not “we have been told to move”. It is: an instrument exists that can void an authorisation at a few hours’ notice, it has been used, it is live for six and a half months of the year, and the probability of it firing is rising. Members are being asked whether to keep accepting that exposure, and if not, what to move to.

3.9 Separately from the law: heat

There is a second, quite different argument for moving, which should not be confused with the regulatory one because it stands or falls on its own evidence: that mid-July in Monegros has become hot enough to change who comes. Families and older participants in particular are reported to have stopped attending for that reason. Whether that effect is real and how large it is are exactly the sort of questions a poll of attendees can answer, and the regulatory case does not depend on the answer either way.

Note also that the table’s low-risk spring weeks sit inside the most sensitive part of the wildlife calendar (see section 6), and the low-risk October weeks bring rainfall roughly double the July figure onto a site with no hard standing. No window is clean on every axis.

Constraints the table does not capture

  • May and early June: local advice is that May is historically the wettest month in Monegros — over 100 litres recorded — and that it is peak harvest season, with tractors and harvesters on the same access roads. These windows have deliberately been kept in the list submitted for assessment, so that information comes back on every option rather than only the pre-selected ones.
  • Late September and October: this is the peak DANA / gota fría window in the Ebro basin. At a site defined by converging barrancos, that is a different kind of risk rather than an absence of risk, and it is the water authority’s domain rather than the fire regime’s.
  • School and academic calendars: moving further into the academic year has been raised as a constraint for participants with children, and for teachers and students. This has not been quantified.
  • Build and strike: the event week is not the whole footprint. Build begins roughly three weeks before and strike ends roughly ten days after, so any window drags a five-to-six week envelope behind it — which is what the authorities assess, and what a June event would push into May.

6. Bird nesting and environmental constraints

Fire is not the only regulated axis. The area holds protected species — notably the lesser kestrel (cernícalo primilla) and Dupont’s lark (alondra ricotí) — whose breeding seasons fall across spring and early summer, and the permitting authority holds a discretionary power to condition or restrict organised events in designated critical areas where they could affect reproduction.

The practical consequence is that moving earlier does not simply trade heat for safety: April to June is the most sensitive part of the wildlife calendar as well as being inside the fire season. We are currently working through the nesting schedules and the mapped protected-area overlaps for both the established site and the proposed new site, which have different exposures and cannot be assumed to behave the same way.

This work is preliminary and unverified. It will form part of the information pack once it is firmer.

7. What we do not know yet

An honest list of the gaps, since this page exists to show the work rather than the conclusion:

  • The authority’s answer. Candidate windows went in on 5 August; a yes/no per window is expected late September or October, most likely after the vote.
  • Verified fire-risk figures. The risk column above is desk research. Replacing it with something defensible is an open task.
  • What attendees would actually do. Polling has not yet been run. A window that is legally and environmentally ideal but which most people cannot attend is not a solution.
  • What other burns say. Outreach is under way; no responses are reflected here yet.
  • Which site. Feasibility work on a possible new site is a separate workstream at an early stage, with a go/no-go requested by 1 December. It interacts with the dates question — the two sites have different environmental exposures and would need separate assessments — but it does not settle it, and the fire regime is not more favourable at the new site.
  • Costs of moving. Supplier availability, staff availability, and the commercial effect of a move on ticket sales have not been quantified.

In one paragraph

A change in Aragonese law in March 2026 means a Roja Plus fire alert now automatically suspends the authorisations the event depends on, at a few hours’ notice, with evacuation rather than shelter-in-place as the authorities’ stated expectation. That alert can be declared at any point between 1 April and 15 October, it has been declared three times in the last two summers — twice around this year’s event — and its frequency is rising. The General Assembly will be asked on 17 September whether to keep the early-July slot regardless, or move — and every alternative window carries its own costs, on wildlife, on weather, on clashes with other events, and on who can actually come. This page is the working state of that assessment. The readable version, with the case for and against each option, will follow in good time before the vote.
