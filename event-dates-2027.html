1. What is actually being decided

The 2026 event ran 7–12 July. Without a decision to the contrary, 2027 would sit in the equivalent early-July window — the working assumption in the planning material is 5–11 July 2027.

The question going to the General Assembly is whether to keep that slot or move to a different window. It is a members’ decision: each member of the association has one vote. The deciding vote is planned for Thursday 17 September 2026, online, and keeping the current dates will be a real option on the ballot.

2. Criteria for date selection

The evidence bearing directly on which window to choose: the wildfire record, heat and rain, the calendar of other European burns, and environmental constraints.

2.1.1 Daily alert levels in each candidate week

Did the alert criteria change?

One important classification change did occur: Roja Plus was added for the 2023 campaign to identify exceptional episodes of extreme danger. That means Red Plus cannot be compared with years before 2023. However, this page starts in 2023, and the Gobierno de Aragón does not identify any subsequent change to the Green / Yellow / Orange / Red thresholds that would explain the sharp rise within this 2023–2026 series. The March 2026 decree changed the legal consequences of Roja Plus for authorised events, not the meteorological calculation of NAPIF.

What appears to drive the levels?

NAPIF is a weather-and-fuel-danger assessment, based on the Canadian Forest Weather Index and adjusted for Aragón using factors including overnight humidity recovery, fire-type analysis, cloud, thunderstorm risk and atmospheric stability. The available evidence therefore supports reading the increase primarily as worse assessed fire-weather and fuel conditions, rather than as an artefact of newly lowered alert thresholds. It does not, by itself, prove how much of the four-year change is attributable to long-term climate change rather than ordinary year-to-year weather variability, drought and fuel condition.

2.3 Candidate windows: heat and rain

The tables below are calculated from daily data for the established-site coordinate, 41.7005615, -0.1363139. They compare matching calendar weeks historically; they are not forecasts for 2027.

Method and sources

Each candidate week is compared with the same seven calendar dates in the twenty complete years 2006–2025. “Typical” is the median daily value; a “hot day” is the 95th percentile daily maximum; a “cool night” is the 5th percentile daily minimum. A wet day has at least 1 mm precipitation. “Weeks with rain” is the proportion of the twenty matching weeks with at least 1 mm total rain; the wet-week figure is the 90th percentile of those twenty weekly totals.

The source is the Open-Meteo Historical Weather API, using its ERA5 / ERA5-Land best-match historical series. Reanalysis is appropriate for consistent comparison at an exact rural coordinate, but it is gridded data rather than a rain gauge on the festival site.

For the current 5–11 July slot, the recent historical median daily high is 34.2°C, with about 2.6 days at or above 35°C and 3.9 nights at or above 20°C in a typical matching week. The mean weekly rain is 6.4 mm, and 65% of matching years had at least 1 mm during the week. May weeks are cooler but not uniformly wetter every week; late September and October have lower heat exposure but materially higher upper-tail weekly rain totals.

Constraints the table does not capture

  • May and early June: local advice is that this overlaps peak harvest activity, with tractors and harvesters using the same access roads. The climate table supplies the rain evidence; the harvest constraint is operational local evidence and has not yet been quantified.
  • Late September and October: the weekly reanalysis shows lower heat but a higher upper tail for accumulated rain. At a site defined by converging barrancos, the consequence of a concentrated storm is a hydraulic question for CHE; weekly averages cannot resolve flash-flood behaviour at the site.
  • School and academic calendars: moving further into the academic year has been raised as a constraint for participants with children, and for teachers and students. This has not been quantified.
  • Build and strike: the event week is not the whole footprint. Build begins roughly three weeks before and strike ends roughly ten days after, so any window drags a five-to-six week envelope behind it — which is what the authorities assess, and what a June event would push into May.

2.5 Bird nesting and environmental constraints

Fire is not the only regulated axis. The area holds protected species — notably the lesser kestrel (cernícalo primilla) and Dupont’s lark (alondra ricotí) — whose breeding seasons fall across spring and early summer, and the permitting authority holds a discretionary power to condition or restrict organised events in designated critical areas where they could affect reproduction.

The practical consequence is that moving earlier does not simply trade heat for safety: April to June is the most sensitive part of the wildlife calendar as well as being inside the fire season. We are currently working through the nesting schedules and the mapped protected-area overlaps for both the established site and the proposed new site, which have different exposures and cannot be assumed to behave the same way.

This work is preliminary and unverified. It will form part of the information pack once it is firmer.

3. Additional information

Background and context: why the question is being asked at all, the open gaps, the timeline, sourcing, and how to take part.

3.1 Why a change of dates is on the table

The issue is not simply July heat. Since 2026, a Roja Plus fire alert can automatically suspend an already-granted event authorisation at very short notice, creating a significant cancellation and evacuation risk.

Roja Plus creates a new regulatory risk

Under Decreto 22/2026, a Roja Plus alert suspends public events and existing authorisations in affected forest areas. The trigger is the official alert level, regardless of actual conditions or how well the event is managed. Vehicle access may also be restricted, affecting build, ingress and egress.

The risk is real and can arise at short notice

In August 2025, an ongoing festival was required to evacuate with approximately 8.5 hours’ notice. For Elsewhere 2026, authorities therefore prepared an extensive evacuation plan involving buses, Guardia Civil, Cruz Roja and receiving villages.

Moving dates reduces — but does not eliminate — the risk

The relevant fire-danger period runs from 1 April to 15 October, and Roja Plus declarations have already affected the site in June, July and August. Moving away from July may therefore lower the probability, but the same regulatory risk remains.

Authorities have also indicated that fire conditions are worsening and restrictions are likely to become stricter over time.

Permitting requires an early decision

Approvals from INAGA, CHE and the Comarca are sequential and can take several months. Dates therefore need to be selected early; waiting until the risk materialises is not a realistic option.

What we are — and are not — saying

No authority has ordered Elsewhere to change its dates, and the event successfully took place in 2026 despite Roja Plus alerts.

The question for members is therefore: do we continue accepting the risk of cancellation or evacuation at short notice, or move to dates where that risk is lower?

Separately, extreme July heat may affect accessibility and attendance, particularly for families and older participants. This is an additional consideration, independent of the regulatory risk.

3.2 What happens in case of an event cancellation

If the event is cancelled before it has started, the situation would likely be similar to what happened during COVID. People who had already purchased a ticket could choose to donate the ticket price to the association or request a refund.

If the event is cancelled after people are already on site, some costs would inevitably need to be covered. This could include, for example, organising buses or other transportation for an evacuation, as well as other costs associated with safely closing the event.

The exact financial impact would therefore depend on the timing and circumstances of the cancellation.

3.3 2026 Timeline

  • 11 March 2026 Decreto 22/2026 approved in Aragón; published in the BOA (no. 53) on 18 March, three and a half months before the 2026 event.
  • 17 June 2026 Meeting with the Comarca de Los Monegros. The comarca reads the new automatic-suspension clause aloud and sets out its evacuation planning.
  • Mid-June 2026 Build begins. A fire roughly an hour away burns toward the site; ash falls on the build. It is brought under control. Evacuation plans — buses, receiving villages, Guardia Civil, Cruz Roja — were by this point genuinely in place, not hypothetical.
  • 26–28 June 2026 Roja Plus declared — three days, during build.
  • 7–12 July 2026 The event runs. The permit holds, nobody is hurt, and there is no full evacuation. Separate Roja Plus declarations apply on 10 July and, during strike, on 12 July, bringing legally binding restrictions on movement, gas cooking and open flame. Ordinary Red alerts continue on many surrounding days.
  • 30 July 2026 Community call. The dates question is announced publicly, alongside the note that “the authorities have also strongly recommended scheduling outside the high-fire-risk period, as operational restrictions grow stricter every year.” Minutes.
  • 3 August 2026 Board votes to move the General Assembly from 3 to 17 September, specifically to allow more time to prepare the dates decision. Minutes.
  • 4 August 2026 Governance working group agrees to prepare a Swiss-style voting booklet for the decision. Minutes.
  • 5 August 2026 The candidate-window document goes to the regional authority for assessment. A response is expected around late September or October — i.e. after the vote.
  • 2 September 2026 GA agenda fixed.
  • 17 September 2026 General Assembly. Members vote.

One consequence of that sequence is worth stating plainly: the authority’s formal yes/no on each candidate window will most likely not be back in time. Whatever members vote on in September will be a decision made under real uncertainty, and the pack should say so rather than pretend otherwise.
