What each session is about

This page tells you what each session will be working on, and what you should walk away with. For the room-by-room timetable, head to the What’s On page.

Sessions marked ⚡️ are for everyone. Parallel sessions run simultaneously, so you’ll need to choose one. All programmed sessions will be hybrid: the link to join the call will be shared in our channels, so join your preferred one via Discord, Telegram or WhatsApp.

How sessions run

Each session has a Topic Lead (holds the scope, keeps the conversation centred on what needs to be worked through) and three facilitation roles: a Facilitator (runs the conversation and queue), a Timekeeper/Secretary (keeps time, notes decisions), and a Relay for hybrid sessions (bridges remote participants into the room). We use hand signals for the speaking queue, agreement, and clarification — and consensus decision-making with blind voting where needed. Ellen and Cupcake will walk everyone through the full framework in the Friday intro session.

Event-Time Ops & Org Hub

Designing the on-site nerve centre for event week: layout, shift structure, radio handover, and the ops board that answers the question “who do I call?”

Working group: Event-Time Ops You'll leave with: An agreed site layout and staffing model for the ops hub

Purpose

During event week there is no clear operational centre. Coordination flows ad hoc between Malfare, Production, and individual coordinators — none of whom have that as their primary function. The result is scope creep on both care and logistics teams at exactly the moment they’re under most pressure. Volunteers arriving for shifts often have no clear place to check in, get oriented, or find out who’s running what.

This session designs the thing. Physical layout, who’s in it, when, and what for.

We’re here to agree three things:

  • Where does the hub sit, what does it contain, and what goes on the walls?
  • Can shift structure, handover rhythm, and protocols be somewhat standardised across event-time departments?
  • How do we restructure event-time roles so operational tasks stop falling on Coordinators and Malfare by default? Proposal on the table: Coordinator (emergencies, authorities, vendors) / Site Ops (physical infrastructure) / Event Ops (human infrastructure) / Departmental shifts (Gate, NoInfo, Ice, Water, Malfare, Welfare, Safer Space, Ohana House, Radios).

Format

Duration: 60 minutes. Opens with a rough site map: where the hub would sit relative to Malfare, Production, and the main compound. 10 minutes or less, this is just the anchor.

Then 40 minutes across the three questions above, working through physical layout first, then operational design. This is a design session, not a discussion on themes.

Final 10 minutes: assign a named owner to each unresolved component. Anything not assigned goes on the post-ALM list with a deadline.

It’d be great to have anyone with direct event-time experience in the room. Especially: Radios, Gate, NoInfo, Ice, Water, LNT, Power, Participant Wellness (all), Production.

What we are not deciding today

  • Specific cabin placement on the 2027 site map (that goes to the Site Design session at 12:00)
  • Specific departmental decisions (e.g. Radio lease vs. purchase)
  • Sound zone policy (separate session at 10:45, after this one)

Output

By the end of the session:

  • Hub placement needs (where in relation to what) and structure needs (what does it physically look like)
  • Shift standardisation model, if agreed
  • Daily rhythm at the hub: morning brief, shift rotation, operating hours
  • Event-time ops roles agreed or proposal advanced with named next steps

Beyond Safehaus: What Should Crew Rest Look Like?

AC cabins covered the heat, people who decompressed off-site came back to strike energised, the second Safehaus never showed up: and we made it. This session decides what we want our crew rest model to look like next year: what we book, what we budget, and how we bust the myth that leaving site means leaving for good.

Working group: Safehaus You'll leave with: A crew rest model and a budget line to put forward for 2027

Purpose

The Safehaus has historically been booked as a default: permit buffer during setup, rest option for burnt-out crew, decompression space after strike. Every year, the Safehaus rent and management takes a lot of time and resources.

In 2026 we didn’t even get the second one on time, and we were fine. This year, AC cabins covered rest on site, and we had the new addition of people who had left off-site to decompress after the event came back to strike energised. That’s a signal that the model can change.

This session looks at what crew rest needs to be in 2027, deliberately designed to offer on-site and off-site spaces that help people work their best without burning out.

Format

Duration: 60 minutes. Opens with a 10-minute framing: what the Safehaus has historically been used for, what changed in 2026, and the headline proposal.

Then 30 minutes across three questions:

  • What does crew rest need to look like across each phase: first crew, setup, pre-event week, event week, and strike?
  • Is there appetite for an organised decompression space between event-end and strike-start? What would it need to be balanced and manageable?
  • What should we try in 2027? Do we keep any Safehaus, or replace it entirely?

Final 20 minutes: agree the model, assign owners.

Output

By the end of the session:

  • A decision on whether Safehaus format continues, is replaced, or is retired for 2027
  • A clear model for volunteer rest across phases
  • Agreed on-site rest infrastructure for 2027
  • One or two owners to take the model into the 2027 planning cycle

Clearing the Air on Sound

Review the updated zone framework and mobile sound rules, agree on consequences for non-compliance, and leave Vidalia with a policy the community can be held to.

Working group: Sound Policy You'll leave with: A ratified sound policy with enforcement mechanisms

Purpose

The Sound Policy Working Group has been gathering community input since September — zone confusion, mobile sound, build-time music, repeat non-compliance. That diagnostic is done.

This session is not about relitigating the problems. It’s about making the calls that have been tabled for too long: what the zones mean, what rules apply to mobile sound and build hours, and what happens when people don’t follow them.

We leave Vidalia with a set of agreed decisions that the WG can turn into a revised policy for community ratification before 2027.

Format

Duration: 60 minutes

Opens with 5 minutes from the WG — a recap of the five live decisions and the slides prepared for this session (circulated in advance).

Then 35 minutes across the decisions, in pairs or small groups, one question per table:

  1. Yellow zone: enforce the living-room standard actively, or redraw the map to match reality?
  2. Mobile sound: what rules apply to art cars and installations — zones, stopping points, curfew alignment?
  3. Build and strike: is there a quiet period for music? Do zone rules apply? What’s the neighbour coordination expectation?
  4. Consequences: what happens to a camp or car that repeatedly ignores the policy?
  5. Who holds it: who owns the policy going forward and how does it go to community ratification?

Final 20 minutes: full group reads back the agreed positions. Facilitator captures live. Anything unresolved goes to the WG with a deadline.

What we are not deciding today

  • The technical spec of the policy (dB limits, measurement equipment) — that’s post-ALM WG work
  • City planning and barrio placement methodology
  • Individual camp or art car appeals

Output

By the end of the session we should have:

  • A decision on Yellow zone — enforce or rezone
  • Agreed rules for mobile sound across the event phases
  • A position on build and strike sound hours
  • A consequence framework the community can be told about and held to
  • A named owner and timeline for community ratification

Designing Our Site: Org Footprint, Structures and Spaces

From toilet design to sleeping cabins, our physical site reflects choices about who we're building for. This session looks at how we make those decisions: what gets built, why, and how the site can work better for everyone on it.

Working group: Site Design You'll leave with: A set of site design principles and a decision process for 2027

Purpose

Every year, decisions about where departments live on site, what gets built, and who those structures serve are made reactively or by default. City Planning, Infrastructure and Power end up making calls with incomplete requirements, and departments don’t know what they need to tell them. Then during setup, under pressure, is when we figure out what doesn’t work.

This session is the beginning of a different process. We want to leave with a shared framework for how we make physical site decisions together as an organisation, and with concrete inputs from every department on what they actually need so City Planning, Infrastructure and Power have something to work with from the start of the event cycle.

We’ll also use the session to address two specific design questions that have come up and need a room to land in: the organisation’s sanitation infrastructure (vulva urinals, toilet design) and how we think about semi-autonomous structures like Offices, Sleeping Cabins, and Departmental Structures in relation to the org’s overall footprint.

The questions we’re here to answer

  1. What does each department need from their physical structure on site in terms of placement: proximity to, far from, power needs?
  2. What does each department need in terms of infrastructure: working space, storage space, shade space, A/C indoor?
  3. How do we decide which structures get built and on what basis, including how offices and sleeping cabins are designed, assigned and managed?
  4. Toilets and urinals placement, vulva urinal design, what can be done to improve?

Format

Duration: 60 minutes

  • 10 min — Departmental needs mapping. Each department or cluster takes 5-10 minutes to answer questions 1 and 2.
  • 15 min — Departmental needs discussion. Share needs and get feedback and ideas from others. Designing the site itself comes after the ALM, in the Site Design working group.
  • 20 min — Structure decisions: how do we make them? Using concrete examples: office cabins, rest/welfare spaces, semi-autonomous structures like Ohanahaus, Bikehaus, NoInfo, Gate. Agree the questions we ask before anything gets allocated or built. Who needs to be in that conversation?
  • 15 min — Sanitation design, vulva urinal proposal and squatting toilet work. Focus is on physical design and not rotas or team composition.

What we are not deciding today

  • Final city layout or container placement for 2027, that’s the post-ALM.
  • LNT shift rotas or ChatChichiPees team composition
  • Compost toilets: parked until new site is confirmed
  • Container packing, power scheduling, strike logistics: covered in Site Systems session

What we want to walk out with

  • A rough departmental requirements map: placement needs, proximity preferences, space and power asks. Something City Planning, Infrastructure and Power can use
  • Agreed questions to ask before any structure (cabin, build, semi-autonomous space) gets allocated or approved
  • A decision or clear next step on the vulva urinal design
  • Commitment to formalise the Site Design working group to have ready the City Planning overall shape, organisation structure ideal placement, departmental and other infrastructures.

MoE/MoN: What Are We Building?

This session looks at MoE/MoN as a living part of Elsewhere: how it's organised, what it needs to thrive, and what we want it to become. We'll align on a budget approach, a team structure, and a plan for 2027.

Working group: MoE/MoN You'll leave with: A governance model and budget outline for MoE/MoN in 2027

Purpose

MoE/MoN is one of Elsewhere’s most visible spaces, but its organisational backbone is thin — currently held by one person, with no formal team structure, unclear budget ownership, and open questions about what the space even wants to be. This session leaves with a concrete proposal: a team structure, a budget idea, and a mandate for 2027.

What we’re not doing

Relitigating what went wrong with equipment this year or workshopping the stretch tent. That’s operational detail for post-ALM.

Questions to resolve

  1. Who holds MoE/MoN as an entity? Is it a working group, a department, a theme camp?
  2. What does a minimum viable team look like (lead, tech, lighting, volunteers)?
  3. What budget does it need, and where does it sit (creativity fund, operations, other)?
  4. What’s the handover/continuity plan so it doesn’t rest on one person?

Proposed outputs

  • A draft team structure (roles, not names)
  • A budget proposal to bring to the Finance
  • A mandate / brief for 2027 that the working group can take forward

Werkhaus: From Volunteer Camp to Org Infrastructure

Werkhaus is core to how the event functions — but it's been held together by last-minute goodwill. This session decides what it actually looks like as a properly governed system: how leads are selected and where it sits within the org.

Working group: Werkhaus You'll leave with: A Werkhaus governance model for 2027

Purpose

Werkhaus is the backbone of setup and strike – the living space for volunteers who keep the site running year-round and across the most demanding phases of the event cycle. But its governance hasn’t kept up: in 2026, the lead role was filled at the last minute, backend logistics like power hookups and water cube orders were handled by other teams without Werkhaus coordination, and event-time fluffing ran entirely off-roster with no presence in Humans.

This session is not a debrief. The problems are known. This is about deciding what Werkhaus looks like as a properly governed part of the organisation before the 2027 cycle kicks off.

Format

Duration: 60 minutes

Opens with 5 minutes from Rory – a quick recap of where things stand and the three decisions on the table.

Then 35 minutes working through the questions, in pairs or small groups:

  1. Governance model: does Werkhaus sit within NCA as a formal department, remain a heavily subsidised barrio with proper org support, or something else?
  2. What’s the process and timeline for selecting/recruiting/confirming a Werkhaus lead for 2027?
  3. Event-time shifts: are Werkhaus maintenance shifts open to any volunteer or should they be taken by Werkhaus members?

Final 20 minutes: full group reads back the agreed positions. Anything unresolved gets a named owner and a deadline.

What we are not deciding today

  • The 2027 Werkhaus budget line (that goes to Finance once the model is agreed)
  • Individual barrio or camp placement questions

What could be discussed if time allows

  • Werkhaus membership criteria and application process

Output

By the end of the session we should have:

  • A chosen governance model for 2027
  • A confirmed coordinator selection process and timeline
  • A decision on event-time shifts to maintain Werkhaus
  • Named owners for each next step, with deadlines

New Site: Purchase and Infrastructure

A structured input session to map what each department can confirm, estimate, or still needs to quote so the business case has real numbers before any decisions are made.

Working group: El Sitio Leads: Pablo / Ward You'll leave with: A department-by-department cost map and a clear list of what still needs quoting

Purpose

We have a potential site purchase on the table. Before any decision can be made (or any proposal put to the community) we need to hear from the people who run operations there.

The goal of this session is to surface what each department knows about what it would mean to permanently own and operate this site: the operational realities, potential costs, risks, and unknowns that should form the base of any serious business case.

We’re here to answer three questions:

  1. What does your department currently spend that is tied to the site, and what would change if we owned it permanently?
  2. What would a permanent site make easier, harder, or possible for the first time?
  3. What risks or unknowns should the business case account for that finance probably hasn’t thought of?

Format

Duration: 90 minutes total (60 min session + 30 min open Q&A)

Each department takes 5-10 minutes on the three questions above. No slides, no spreadsheet. Then we share answers, try to capture key points live and draw out specifics.

Open Q&A on the New Site: 30 minutes. If we run over on the session, the Q&A can be shortened and people can continue the conversation on the Discord channel.

Output: Enough real operational knowledge to go get quotes and ground the numbers, so the El Sitio working group can start drafting a business case.

What we are not deciding today

  • Whether to buy the site
  • Any numbers, the business case comes later

What could be discussed if time allows

  • How to raise the money or structure community contributions
  • Crowdfunding mechanics

Post-session survey

Not everyone who should weigh in will be in the room. After the session a short survey goes out to everyone so those who weren’t there can respond, and those who were can add anything they forgot or have had more time to think through.

The survey mirrors the three questions above and feeds into the same working document, so the El Sitio working group has a full picture to build from.
