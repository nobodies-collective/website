What this is. A sync note from a working conversation between the people named above — not the minutes of a working-group meeting: no agenda was circulated and nobody else was invited. It records only the parts of the conversation that concern the 2027 event-dates decision; unrelated topics and personal remarks are left out. Published with the agreement of the participants, as part of the Event Dates working group’s commitment to keep its work in the open.

A one-hour working call to scope the information pack and the process for the community decision on the 2027 event dates: what the “booklet” should contain, which criteria to score each candidate week against, who researches what, and what the vote is — and is not — deciding.

1. What the vote is, and is not

Agreed, and to be stated at the top of the information page: this is not a go / no-go vote on 2027, and not a vote on the new site versus the old site. Whichever site the event is on, which week should it be? A go / no-go question would need its own information pack and, if anyone wants to raise it, should come to the General Assembly as a separate proposal rather than a footnote.

The vote is on a ranked list of candidate weeks. The winning week is confirmed only once the environmental authority (INAGA) approves it — if it does not, the next-ranked approved week applies. The current early-July week remains on the ballot.

2. Why the question is being put to the members

Daniel T summarised the board’s reasoning: a substantial part of the board believes the event has at best even odds of surviving on its current dates — in 2026 there were two “Roja Plus” alerts, one during the event, and by the 16 June authorities’ meeting there were already fires most days. Heat is a secondary reason. Because the change is contentious and long-lasting, the board wants the members to decide rather than deciding by itself.

Céline S added a practical point: an event placed after a bad fire season will get less institutional goodwill than one placed before it.

3. Criteria for the information table

Six columns, red / amber / green per week, each cell expandable to its detail and sources:

  • Wildfire / cancellation risk — the main criterion. The current scale was produced with AI-assisted research and needs to be re-derived from traceable sources before publication.
  • Wildlife / bird nesting — INAGA permit question, to be assessed for both sites.
  • Weather — temperature and rain (already compiled).
  • Other burns — their 2027 dates and their reaction to each candidate week.
  • School holidays in Europe — informational only; very few children attend and the effect on parents cuts both ways.
  • Board recommendation — not a single recommended date: each board member votes yes / no per week, the count is published with reasons, purely as information.

Two disclaimers were agreed: many factors in people’s own decision to attend cannot be assessed; and the effect of other burns’ dates and school holidays on ticket sales could go either way.

4. Rain, harvest and the May question

A local recommendation advised dropping May and early June (wettest month, harvest traffic on the access roads). Agreed position: keep those weeks in the INAGA request and on the ballot, and give members the information — the roads concern applies mainly to the current site, and the rain data show more wet days but not much more rain.

5. Page structure

Loosely modelled on the Swiss voting booklet: a short summary of why a change is being considered, the criteria table, expandable detail, sources linked throughout, and short enough to read in one sitting. Working data stays in a shared spreadsheet (comment access for the community; edit access for the researchers) with the finished content moved to the website.
