Governance Meeting — Fourth Session

Date: Monday 31 August 2026, video call
Present (video call): Celine V (facilitator), Daniel T, Anna T. Apologies from Peter K and Kate B.

The fourth governance meeting. The session settled the group’s own status under the new working-group framework, reviewed the proposed voting method for the event-dates decision, and agreed to bring the board-role and coordinator-role questions to the Annual Leadership Meeting as structured workshops.

1. Group Membership

Kate B has stepped back from the governance group for now, citing the demands of a new job, and Bambi is similarly short on time. Daniel T has offered to meet Kate for a coffee; Celine V will also check in. The door stays open: under the working-group framework, anyone can start or join governance work at any time.

2. Registering as a Working Group

Anna T repeated her definitional point from the last community call: under the board’s framework, a working group forms around a specific problem, runs for a defined period, and presents an outcome — which describes the event-dates work, but not the standing bi-weekly governance conversation. Daniel T reported he has registered the event-dates working group accordingly, with himself and Celine V as coordinators.

After discussion, the group agreed the standing conversation should also register: as a “seedbed” working group whose purpose is to identify governance shortcomings and either resolve them directly or spin up focused working groups, and whose output is governance proposals or further working groups. Anna T’s concern that registering might close the membership was addressed by making openness explicit: meetings remain open to anyone who wants to contribute, and registration simply gives the work a visible official channel — a place on the website and published minutes. Celine V will check with previous participants about who wants to be named as a member and submit the application within the week.

A related tidy-up: the organisation currently has a governance “team”/“department”, but if teams are groups organising the event, governance is not one. The working group may propose retiring that structure.

3. Voting Mechanism: Ranked Pairs

The group reviewed the proposed ranked-pairs voting method for the event-dates decision: every pair of options is compared head-to-head, and the option winning the most pairwise comparisons wins. This avoids the known failure of instant-runoff methods, where a broadly popular consensus option can be eliminated early for lacking first-choice votes, and includes a defined tie-break for circular deadlocks. Celine V endorsed the method and asked that it be presented to the board before use; Daniel T will prepare a short presentation with a simple example. The method can still be changed if someone makes a strong argument before the vote opens.

The ranked-voting functionality is being built into the Humans app: by design, nobody — including admins — can see results until the vote closes, and a closed vote cannot be reopened.

The information website has been updated following last week’s call and is essentially ready. The broad consultation poll has passed 750 responses; in line with the board’s earlier decision, interim standings are not published while the consultation runs. One person has so far registered to speak about the dates at this week’s community call; Daniel T will post a reminder inviting others to sign up.

5. Elections, Quorum, and the Statutes

Interest is growing in the governance channel’s election-reflection thread, where Doreen has offered to work on holding board elections sooner than the scheduled cycle — a natural candidate for a focused working group. Points from the discussion:

  • Timing. The board has previously minuted its wish for a larger, more diverse board, but the statutes do not currently permit an election before the scheduled cycle, so a statute change must come first. One sketch: statute change at an October General Assembly, applications through November, elections in early December — new members in place before next year’s event preparation. Yearly staggered elections were floated as one possible pattern.
  • Quorum. Growing the board without adjusting quorum rules risks deadlock. A minimal safeguard was sketched: if a regular board meeting fails to reach quorum, the following week’s attendance becomes the temporary quorum until members return.
  • Well-structured votes. The statutes fix a General Assembly’s agenda 15 days in advance but say nothing about voting mechanisms or what information must be presented under an agenda item — a gap a focused working group could address.

6. Board Diversity — and What Is the Board For?

Several people have told Anna T that the desire for earlier elections is really a desire for diversity: the current board is all men. Daniel T agreed and would like that to change. But Anna T pressed a prior question: the community holds visibly different ideas about what the board’s role and powers actually are — electing more people onto a body whose function is undefined resolves little. The community carries both an inherited concept of the board as “the top” and a desire not to be a top-down organisation; if the board is to be the minimal interface to institutions that require a legal entity, the community needs to decide where real decision-making does live.

Daniel T pointed to his board application, which argued for exactly this — keep the board roles simple, documented, and replaceable, and move power toward the community — and noted he would prefer the pressure to keep it that way to come from the community rather than the board itself. Agreed: bring this to the ALM as one or more structured workshops. Anna T will draft a framing document; Celine V will design a workshop format that can produce decisions with a large group.

7. Coordinator Roles

The same gap exists one level down: coordinators today hold their roles through no consistent process, and are perceived in sharply different ways by the people they work with. There is no defined way to select coordinators, no succession process, no red-flag mechanism when something is not working, and no shared answer to how many coordinators are useful or which roles should be coordinator roles. These questions — selection, succession, scope, accountability, and the communication line that lets the community know each area is on track before the event — go into the ALM framing document alongside the board question.

8. Explaining the Association: Newsletter

Raised by Peter K via the shared list: many people, including leads, still find the membership structure unclear — some who meet the criteria to become an asociado wrongly believe they cannot apply, and the difference between colaborador and asociado is widely misunderstood. Agreed: explain it repeatedly through multiple channels — a newsletter item, and a short presentation with questions at a community call.

At minimum, an announcement should confirm the ALM is October, with dates fixed and organisation under way. Celine V offered organisational support, and Daniel T has passed the offer to the ALM’s main coordinator.
