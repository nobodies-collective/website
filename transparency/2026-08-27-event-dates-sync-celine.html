A short call to walk through the event-dates information page before it is frozen for the vote: what is still missing, what can be cut, and how the vote will be run.

1. Survey status

The community survey stood at just over 630 responses, roughly half of them from identified, unique accounts. The shape of the preferences has not changed since the first hundred responses, and it is the same whether one looks at identified, anonymous or combined answers — a useful answer to anyone who worries that 600 people is too small a sample. Interim results are deliberately withheld until the survey closes, to avoid influencing late voters; they will be added to this note afterwards.

A lesson for the next such survey: “I will not come” was used by some people to mean “I don’t like this date”. Next time, two separate questions — will you come? and do you like this date? — so the two signals can be told apart.

Cuts agreed: the “what we do not know yet” chapter (repetitive) and the long timeline, which becomes an expandable section — kept because seeing how the decision was reached, with links to the minutes, is part of what makes the result trustworthy.

3. Cancellation versus evacuation

A community question — what happens to tickets if the event is cancelled, and is it insured? — led to a distinction that will be written up as a short paragraph: no insurer would cover an event in fire season, so nobody should count on that. The realistic risks are refusal of the permit, which would be known well before ticket sales open in January (the application starts before the end of the year), and evacuation during the event, which is the scenario the early-July dates expose us to. The nearest precedent is the 2020 cancellation, months ahead, with refunds offered to whoever asked. The board also plans a resale-pool ticket model that lets early buyers return a ticket to the pool and recover their money.

4. How the vote will run

Five to seven candidate weeks, probably including the current one (the board felt omitting it would look bad), with weeks the whole board considers unworkable left off. Ranked-choice: if no week has more than half the first preferences, the lowest is eliminated and its votes move to those voters’ next choice, until one does; the count can be re-run with a week removed if the permit is refused. Results will be announced at the 3 September community call, with the survey left open until 4 September for latecomers. Two survey features are worth building for next time: a ranked-choice question type and the two-axis grid above.
