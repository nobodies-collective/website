Elections Working Group

Status: agreed at the Annual Leadership Meeting on 17 October 2026; getting organised
Purpose. To develop concrete proposals for board election reform — for example staggering board terms so that not everyone rotates at once, and broadening who stands for the board.
Expected output. Proposals for the General Assembly to vote on. Changes to how the board is elected are statute amendments and need a two-thirds majority of the General Assembly.

Board Meeting — ALM Highlights, Board Changes and One Election

Seven of eight board members, so quorum was present. Ben D and Victor P announced they will resign from the board, and the board unanimously accepted Michelle’s resignation notice of 5 September, subject to a seven-day window. 17 October 2026ALM session

ALM — Getting Into and Out of Roles: board composition

Board selection is set by the statutes, and any amendment needs a two-thirds vote of the General Assembly. Staggering board terms and board diversity were raised and deferred. Agreed: a working group, starting the following Monday, will develop concrete proposals for board election reform for the General Assembly to vote on.
5 October 2026Board meeting

Board elections: timing

The timing of the next board election, in view of a possible vacancy. An election needs between 45 and 60 days’ notice, and the board wanted the organisational-structure work to clarify the board’s role first.

Legal advice came back on elections and quorum: the next election can be moved. Board members were to connect with community members interested in an elections working group.
27 August 2026Community call

The idea is raised

The board would like an election to grow and diversify the board, but the statutes only allow one every two years unless someone steps down; a working group could propose staggered annual elections with two-year terms.
