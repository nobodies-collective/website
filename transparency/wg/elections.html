Elections Working Group

Expected output. The board election, held within the period the statutes require.

About this election

This section is for anyone curious about what is happening, and for anyone thinking about standing for the board. The working group will add to it as decisions are made.

Why there is an election

At the board meeting of 19 October 2026, the resignations of three board members — Ben D, Victor P and Michelle — were formally accepted. Ben D announced it on Discord the following day. Under the association’s statutes, an election for the vacant seats must be held between 45 and 60 days after the resignations: that is, between 3 and 18 December 2026. The board will set the exact date at its meeting on Monday 26 October.

The roles

Three roles are to be filled, and the number cannot be increased without changing the statutes:

  • Secretary (one seat)
  • Vocal (two seats; non-executive board members)

At the association’s first election, all eight applicants were elected, which is why the board currently has eight members.

What the board decides

If you are thinking about standing, it helps to know what the job actually involves. Day-to-day work on the event — vendors, build, shifts, programme — belongs to the production team, coordinators and departments. The board’s work is governance: the decisions only the association’s governing body can take, and decisions about the board itself. These examples come from this year’s board meetings; each links to the minutes for the full context.

  • Calling general assemblies. Calling a general assembly is a formal board act and needs quorum. The board called the first one, on 30 July, moved its date on 3 August to allow more time to prepare, and on 26 October called the 16 December assembly that includes this election, with the statutory notice to every member.
  • Shaping what members vote on. Members make the decision; the board frames the question. For the 2027 event-dates vote, the board decided that the ballot would not include a “no event” option, since the statutes give members another route for that (10 August). It chose the voting method (31 August), and it set the shortlist of weeks, publishing each board member’s position on each one (7 September).
  • Elections and the board’s own membership. The board asked legal counsel whether an election could be held outside the two-year cycle (10 August; answer received 31 August). It accepted resignations, agreed to hold one election for all the vacated seats, and the president designated who takes on the secretary’s duties in the meantime (19 October).
  • The rules the association runs by. Quorum rules (31 August), staggered board terms and a regular December assembly for the accounts and budget (21 September), and how to approach amending the statutes (26 October). Statute changes are decided by members at a general assembly; the board decides what to propose and when.
  • Delegating authority, and its limits. Setting up a finance team able to approve emergency expenses within agreed limits, and authorising volunteer coordination to appoint coordinators where departments lacked them (18 May). Whether coordinators could execute payments within a board-approved budget (14 September). Letting the secretary register working groups directly, with the board involved only on specific grounds (31 August).
  • Deciding what is not a board matter. Keeping the new-site initiative as a separate stream from the association (3 August). Leaving a team’s post-event learning to that team (21 September). Making clear that selecting coordinators is not the board’s role (26 October).
  • Spending beyond the board’s mandate. Some decisions are too big for the board alone: buying land, for example, would need a mandate from the general assembly (26 October).
  • Conflicts of interest. When a board member was contracted for paid operational work, the board voted on it in two separate meetings with the member abstaining, took professional advice, and set a wider compensation policy aside for a working group or general assembly (30 July).
  • Admitting members. Each application to become an asociado needs the votes of half the board (3 August), and the board applies, and sometimes has to interpret, the published membership criteria (19 October).
  • The association’s public positions. How to answer a public enquiry about children at an event that is also sex-positive: the relevant departments prepare the substance, and the board reviews it (14 September). How to answer requests that the association take a political position: it does not (21 September).
  • Reporting to members. When and how to share the event’s financial results with asociados (31 August, 21 September).

A board member also carries the routine duties behind these decisions: attending meetings so there is quorum, voting on membership applications, and approving minutes.

How it may work

The format of this year’s election has not yet been decided; proposing it is this working group’s first job. For reference, at the first election:

  • Candidates submitted a written application answering a set of questions. Some also sent a designed PDF alongside their text answers, and there was an optional video.
  • The vote used a ranked-choice method.

If there are more than three candidates this time, it will be a competitive election decided by a ranked-choice vote, with the counting method confirmed in advance. If there are three or fewer, a candidate is elected unless more than 50% of the votes reject their candidacy.

The first election’s application asked whether people were applying individually or as part of a team. The idea was to let several people link their applications, but it was never worked out in detail, and no team applications happened in practice. There is no current information on team applications until the working group defines it.

Previous applications

Get involved or ask a question

Questions are welcome in the #elections channel on Discord. Anyone can join the working group to help organise the election. The main requirement is that you are not planning to stand yourself, as that would be a conflict of interest. Daniel T set the group up so that questions have a natural place to land, and will gladly hand the coordination to anyone who wants to lead it.
