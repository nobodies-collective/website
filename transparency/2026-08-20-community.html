A lighter-agenda call that turned into a substantive consultation: a board-elections update, the proposed working-groups framework, the El Sitio (new site) working group going public, the coordinators’ debrief cycle, and an extended community discussion on how the 2027 event-dates decision should be made.

1. Board Elections Update (Pablo V)

  • Following questions on Discord about how the board works and how people can join: the board currently has eight members; the statutes allow up to 13.
  • Per legal advice, new members can only be added at an election, and the next scheduled election is January 2028 (two years after the first). There is currently no mechanism for an earlier election — unless a board member steps down or is removed by the General Assembly, which would trigger an extraordinary election. Anyone can run for the board.
  • Pablo has asked the lawyers whether a statute amendment could allow elections to be called earlier; answer pending — update at a future call.
  • A related issue: quorum. Decisions need half-plus-one of board members, and the statutes have no escape mechanism if quorum repeatedly can’t be reached. Pablo intends to propose a statute amendment addressing quorum blocks (potentially bundled with easier election-calling).
  • Beyond elections, Pablo is exploring other ways for people to get involved with board work (e.g. observer roles) — ideas to be brought to a future board meeting and community call.

2. Working Groups Framework (Daniel T)

  • The board discussed working groups at length on Monday (see the board minutes on this transparency page) and reached a direction, not yet voted on: a registry model. Anyone who wants to organise people around a topic can register a working group with the secretary, and gets a Discord channel plus a page on the website for minutes and resources.
  • Registration is not a board endorsement — it doesn’t mean the board has decided you’re the right people for the topic; it means you’ve decided to work on it and are doing so visibly.
  • Working groups can bring their output anywhere appropriate: community calls, a department, a board proposal, or a General Assembly proposal.
  • Guidance rather than gatekeeping: engage the people you’re impacting, be transparent, be inclusive, make it easy for others to find and join the group.
  • The board hopes to pass the enabling resolution at Monday’s board meeting, then set up channels and pages for the first working groups. Questions and opinions to the governance channel on Discord.

3. El Sitio — New Site Working Group (Pablo V)

The group that has been working on the new site for months is becoming the first worked example of the framework — “a working group is a bit like a barrio: get together with a few people and you can make one.”

  • Website: elsitio.kimi.page — the up-to-date presentation, project status, and FAQ (commercially sensitive negotiation details excluded).
  • Status: feasibility stage. One parcel of land is secured; negotiations continue on the other. Candidate event dates have been submitted to the authorities, who are away until September — so an event there in September is almost certainly not happening; October is a maybe.
  • The plan: lock in terms to establish clear costs; if possible run a trial event (roughly 100–500 people); then bring a proposal to a binding community-wide vote before NCA commits to purchasing land — that gate is where a working group’s output becomes an organisational decision.
  • Upcoming projects: land agreements, finding a co-buyer, due diligence, species protection. Offers of help welcome.
  • Open calls every other Wednesday at 18:00 (Spain), starting Wednesday 26 August — calendar link and Zoom on the website and Discord.
  • Pablo noted he acts in this group as a coordinator/point of contact, not as a board member. The framework will likely formalise one or two coordinators per working group — points of contact, not leads.

5. 2027 Event Dates — Survey Status & Decision-Process Consultation

Survey status (Daniel T): the community survey (Google Form + Humans app, launched together to avoid poll fatigue) is in final preparation — target is to send it out by the end of the weekend. It will cover date preferences, dates people can’t attend, and build/strike availability, with a pre-selected set of plausible dates to keep the form manageable. The survey is input, not the binding vote.

Why dates are moving (recap): under the decreto passed in March, all permits are automatically suspended by law during Roja Plus fire alerts — and by that law, this year’s event should have been cancelled three times. It went ahead only because of intense discussions with the government of Aragón, with the Comarca and the mayor on the event’s side — leniency that cannot be counted on next year, after the authorities strongly implied the event should move. This wasn’t theoretical: during build, a fire was heading towards the site and ash was falling on it; it happened not to come close enough to force an evacuation. The board believes that staying in the same window carries a more-than-50% chance of cancellation next year. Alert criteria haven’t changed year-on-year; the alerts themselves have spread and started earlier — no green periods at all this year. Full data (fire alerts, weather, bird-nesting schedules) on the 2027 Dates page.

Permits (Anna T’s point): community preference is one input; permits are another. INAGA (the environmental agency) assesses factors like bird-nesting seasons and typically takes one to three months to respond; clearance requests covering the candidate range have been submitted. Voting before the INAGA report — with ranked preferences — means that if the top date is ruled out, the next-ranked permitted date applies without a new process. Early clarity matters: participants, suppliers, and other burns all plan around these dates.

The open question Daniel put to the call: how should the final decision be made? (1) The board decides, with surveys as advisory input; (2) the board pre-selects viable dates from the survey data and the asociados make a binding vote among them; (3) the asociados vote on the full range of sensible dates, binding on the board.

Community input, in summary:

  • João T: the discussion before the vote matters as much as the vote. The go/no-go process suffered from concerns surfacing last-minute with no time to discuss them. Proposed dedicated space — a call and/or Discord threads — where people can make the case for specific dates before voting; supportive of a community decision provided turnout is meaningful and voters are informed.
  • Condor: fine either way on board vs. members, as long as there’s an opportunity to discuss beforehand; suggested a board recommendation accompanying any member vote (as at many AGMs); keep the number of options small — the name vote showed what too many choices does. The two-level nature of the vote (big-picture viability vs. personal convenience) is probably why some expect the board to filter options.
  • Vishal: add free-text fields to the survey — preferred date and why, unwanted date and why — then summarise the reasons (AI can help) so the eventual voters see the full range of arguments without a hundred people repeating them live.
  • Benoit: the asociados should decide — deferring to the board gives up ownership and invites “the board did this” resentment later; barrio leads especially need to own the outcome. Also suggested voting on ranges (early/late month) rather than exact dates, and echoed that capturing reasons matters — many are structural (fire risk, event character, school holidays) rather than personal.
  • Azzurro: most topics suit a member vote, but some are better decided by the board where it has materially more insight or carries the workload consequences.
  • Imanol: make it data-driven — the survey should establish availability (build, event, strike) per candidate window, and the choice should sit where attendance capacity and weather/fire data best overlap.
  • Tancredi T: whoever decides, a working group should own the preparation — compiling the information package, gathering opinions, chasing the discussion — so the informed-vote workload doesn’t keep falling on the same people.
  • Ellen: asked whether “no change” is an option — answer: it will appear in the survey; whether it appears in a final vote depends on the process chosen, and the board would strongly advise against it on fire-risk grounds. (A null result is always possible under ranked voting: if no option clears 50% at any rank, all options are rejected.) Also asked about the asociado application cutoff — there is none beyond being approved before the vote; approval takes a few days, so apply early.
  • Olivier (Ankhor): asked why the board would pre-select dates at all. Daniel’s answer: partly voter consideration (nobody wants another 26-option ranked ballot), partly a fear some hold that an unfiltered vote picks a non-viable date — a fear Daniel said he doesn’t share, citing the Swiss model: frequent votes work when voters get good information, and with the data pages plus AI-assisted summaries that standard is reachable.

In the chat, meanwhile: Pablo V (before signing off) proposed a concrete sequence — take the current report, send the survey as information-gathering, then prepare an Assembly voting-ready version with board recommendations for the top 2–3 choices, with deep context (“As a board member I want the GA to settle this vote”). René B strongly felt it should be put to a vote — “that is why we are an association: to move away from top-down decision-making and giving the community a voice” — the message Daniel read aloud on the call. Inge W would love the community to vote, with pros/cons per date. Humberto noted several festivals were cancelled in August for heat — moving is clearly in the community’s interest. Azzurro added that free campers who want to get involved will get involved — the job is to create the possibility. Condor cautioned that referendums on complex issues fail when voters lack information — fine with asociados deciding, not a whole-community binding vote. And Ellen K offered a barrio-support push to make sure every barrio lead who wants to be an asociado has applied — taken up on the spot (“Yes please do!”).

Daniel’s own position (stated as personal, not board opinion): the community should be in charge of this event, and the way a community learns to use its power is by using it — but he’d heard feedback that he may be moving too fast, which is why he asked. His reading of the call: the middle option — board pre-selects viable dates, asociados vote among them — is emerging as the likely shape, possibly with board support levels shown per date.

Membership reminder: people who were leads at related events in the region qualify to apply as asociados even if they weren’t at this year’s event — a misconception surfaced just today. If that’s you or someone you know, apply.

5. Fechas del evento 2027 — Estado de la encuesta y consulta sobre el proceso de decisión

Estado de la encuesta (Daniel T): la encuesta comunitaria (Google Form + app Humans, lanzadas a la vez para evitar fatiga de encuestas) está en preparación final — objetivo: enviarla antes del final del fin de semana. Cubrirá preferencias de fechas, fechas imposibles y disponibilidad para montaje/desmontaje, con un conjunto preseleccionado de fechas plausibles. La encuesta es información de entrada, no la votación vinculante.

Por qué se mueven las fechas (resumen): según el decreto aprobado en marzo, todos los permisos quedan automáticamente suspendidos por ley durante las alertas Roja Plus — y según esa ley, el evento de este año debería haberse cancelado tres veces. Salió adelante solo gracias a intensas conversaciones con el Gobierno de Aragón, con la Comarca y el alcalde del lado del evento — una tolerancia con la que no se puede contar el año que viene, después de que las autoridades sugirieran con claridad que el evento debería moverse. No fue algo teórico: durante el montaje, un incendio se dirigía hacia el terreno y caía ceniza sobre él; simplemente no llegó lo bastante cerca como para forzar una evacuación. La junta cree que quedarse en la misma ventana conlleva una probabilidad de cancelación superior al 50% el próximo año. Los criterios de alerta no han cambiado de un año a otro; las alertas se han extendido y empiezan antes — este año no ha habido periodos verdes. Todos los datos en la página de Fechas 2027.

Permisos (apunte de Anna T): la preferencia comunitaria es un factor; los permisos, otro. INAGA (la agencia ambiental) evalúa factores como las épocas de nidificación y suele tardar de uno a tres meses; ya se han presentado solicitudes que cubren el rango de fechas candidatas. Votar antes del informe de INAGA — con preferencias ordenadas — significa que si la primera fecha queda descartada, se aplica la siguiente fecha permitida sin un proceso nuevo.

La pregunta abierta que Daniel planteó: ¿cómo debe tomarse la decisión final? (1) Decide la junta, con las encuestas como consulta; (2) la junta preselecciona fechas viables y los asociados votan de forma vinculante entre ellas; (3) los asociados votan sobre el rango completo de fechas razonables, con carácter vinculante.

Aportaciones de la comunidad, en resumen:

  • João T: el debate previo importa tanto como el voto. El proceso del go/no-go sufrió porque las objeciones aparecieron en el último momento sin tiempo para discutirlas. Propone espacios dedicados — una llamada y/o hilos de Discord — donde defender fechas concretas antes de votar.

También sugirió votar por rangos (principios/finales de mes) y recalcó que capturar los motivos importa.
  • Azzurro: la mayoría de temas encajan con voto de los miembros, pero algunos los decide mejor la junta cuando tiene mucho más contexto o carga con las consecuencias de trabajo.

5. Termine 2027 — Umfrage-Status & Konsultation zum Entscheidungsprozess

Umfrage-Status (Daniel T): Die Community-Umfrage (Google Form + Humans-App, gleichzeitig gestartet, um Umfragemüdigkeit zu vermeiden) ist in der Endvorbereitung — Ziel: Versand bis Ende des Wochenendes. Abgefragt werden Terminpräferenzen, unmögliche Termine und Verfügbarkeit für Auf-/Abbau, mit einer vorausgewählten Menge plausibler Termine. Die Umfrage ist Input, nicht die bindende Abstimmung.

Warum die Termine verschoben werden (Rekap): Nach dem im März verabschiedeten Dekret werden alle Genehmigungen während Roja-Plus-Brandalarmen per Gesetz automatisch ausgesetzt — und nach diesem Gesetz hätte das diesjährige Event dreimal abgesagt werden müssen. Es fand nur statt dank intensiver Gespräche mit der Regierung von Aragonien, mit der Comarca und dem Bürgermeister auf Seiten des Events — eine Nachsicht, auf die man nächstes Jahr nicht zählen kann, nachdem die Behörden deutlich nahegelegt haben, das Event zu verlegen. Das war nicht theoretisch: Während des Aufbaus bewegte sich ein Feuer auf das Gelände zu, und Asche fiel darauf; es kam schlicht nicht nah genug, um eine Evakuierung zu erzwingen. Der Vorstand schätzt, dass ein Verbleib im selben Zeitfenster eine Absagewahrscheinlichkeit von über 50% für nächstes Jahr bedeutet. Die Alarmkriterien haben sich nicht geändert; die Alarme selbst sind ausgedehnter und beginnen früher — dieses Jahr gab es gar keine grünen Phasen. Alle Daten auf der Seite Termine 2027.

Genehmigungen (Anna Ts Punkt): Die Community-Präferenz ist ein Faktor; Genehmigungen sind ein anderer. INAGA (die Umweltbehörde) bewertet u. a. Brutzeiten und braucht meist ein bis drei Monate; Anfragen für den Kandidatenzeitraum sind eingereicht. Vor dem INAGA-Bericht abzustimmen — mit Rangfolge — heißt: Fällt der Spitzentermin weg, gilt der nächstplatzierte zulässige Termin ohne neuen Prozess.

Die offene Frage, die Daniel stellte: Wie soll die finale Entscheidung fallen? (1) Der Vorstand entscheidet, Umfragen sind beratend; (2) der Vorstand wählt praktikable Termine vor und die Asociados stimmen bindend darüber ab; (3) die Asociados stimmen bindend über die gesamte sinnvolle Terminspanne ab.

  • Vishal: Freitextfelder in die Umfrage — Wunschtermin und warum, unerwünschter Termin und warum — und die Gründe dann zusammenfassen (KI kann helfen).
  • Benoit: Die Asociados sollen entscheiden — die Entscheidung dem Vorstand zu überlassen, gibt Verantwortung ab und lädt später zu „der Vorstand war’s“-Ressentiment ein; gerade Barrio-Leads müssen die Entscheidung als ihre eigene empfinden. Schlägt auch Zeiträume (Monatsanfang/-ende) statt exakter Daten vor.
  • Azzurro: Die meisten Themen passen zur Mitgliederabstimmung, aber manche entscheidet der Vorstand besser, wenn er deutlich mehr Kontext hat oder die Arbeitslast trägt.
