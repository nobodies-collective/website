Board Meeting

Date: Monday 7 September 2026, evening (~65 min)
Board present: Daniel T (president), Pablo V (vice-president), Chris, Victor P · Absent: Peter D (treasurer, travelling), Ward D, Ben D, Michelle · Observers: none (Daniela, production coordinator, sent apologies) Four of the eight board members attended, so no quorum was present. The single substantive agenda item — fixing the shortlist of weeks for the asociado vote on 2027 event dates, and recording each board member’s position on each week — was worked through by those present. The remaining board members are being asked to record their positions on the same list within a day, so that the full board’s view can be included in the vote package. Ben D’s item on asociado approvals was deferred in his absence; the previous meeting’s minutes were not validated for the same reason.

A short, single-topic meeting: the board read the published survey together, argued its way to a shortlist of eight weeks for the asociado vote — three around the early-June peak, three around the mid-September peak, the current early-July week as the status quo, and 6–12 September added so that nobody has to ask why it was left out — and then went through the list week by week, each board member stating a for/against position on viability and risk and the reasons for it. These minutes record both the route to the shortlist and the reasoning, because they are the board’s main input into the vote.

1. Check-in and agenda

  • Check-in done. Agenda confirmed: one item, event dates. No production update (Daniela absent); minutes of the previous meeting not validated (Ben absent).

2. What the board was deciding, and why

  • Following the 3 September community call, the approach with the broadest support was the middle one of the three options presented there: the board pre-selects a shortlist of weeks, and asociados choose among them in a ranked vote — rather than either a vote on every surveyed week (the “26-option” experience of the event-name vote) or a decision taken by the board alone.
  • What the call left open was the size of the shortlist (five or seven?) and whether the current early-July week counts inside that number or is added on top.
  • The board also committed to publishing how each board member votes on each shortlisted week, as part of the information pack. Daniel proposed doing this as a for/against position per week rather than a ranking: with seven or eight voters a ranking carries little information, whereas “two board members support this week, all eight support that one” is useful to members — including where the board does not believe a week is a good idea.

3. How the shortlist was arrived at

  1. Reading the survey. Daniel walked through the published survey results (see the survey results page). Two clear peaks of availability: early June, centred on 7–13 June (67% of all respondents could come; 73% of identified respondents), and mid-September, centred on 13–19 September (62%; 61% identified, 63% anonymous). Interest falls away in May, in late June and, sharply, in early July (5–11 July: 47% overall, 36% among anonymous respondents — Daniel’s reading being that identified respondents are the committed core who “will come whatever the date”, which is how the survey was phrased), and again into October. His opening proposal: three weeks around each peak, plus the current week — six plus one — and nothing from May, late June or October, where community interest is visibly lower.
  2. An alternative: list everything, mark favourites. Pablo V asked whether the intent was to filter to a shortlist or to list all weeks and mark the board’s favourites — his recollection of the community call was that people wanted to see the board’s opinion, not necessarily a narrowed ballot. His sketch: all weeks on the ballot, with four or five marked as board favourites (two in June, two in September) and the current week marked “current” rather than “favourite”, so that a preference for no change can still be expressed. On his analysis, five weeks (the two peaks plus the current one) would already cover about 94% of weighted respondents. Daniel’s recollection of the call was that pre-selection was the option people leaned towards and nobody had asked to vote on everything; Pablo accepted that, noting that if pre-selecting, seven has a marginal benefit over five.
  3. Can members add a week during the vote? Chris asked whether someone could bring a motion to add a week. No: once a vote opens on Humans it is a fixed package — no edits, no added options — and a week added late would be unknown to earlier voters and lose by default. Daniel’s view, which Victor P shared (you choose among the candidates that are standing; you don’t add one at the ballot box), is that the past weeks of consultation, calls and summaries were the period for putting options on the table. Chris accepted this for the dates vote, noting that for other kinds of vote the association will eventually need a way for members to bring proposals.
  4. How many? Given pre-selection, Chris preferred more than four options; Victor P and Daniel both settled on around seven. Daniel noted that, unlike the event-name vote, members don’t have to rank every option — unranked weeks count as joint last — so a slightly longer list is not a burden.
  5. Balance between the two peaks. Daniel wanted the same number of weeks in each cluster. Ranked Pairs handles vote-splitting (three September weeks against one June week would not disadvantage September), but an unbalanced list would still look unfair. Victor P’s own analysis, run on an earlier cut of the data, had produced May + two Junes + two Septembers, which he felt biased the list earlier; he agreed to swap for balance.
  6. Which weeks, exactly. Around the June peak, the third slot was between 24–30 May and 14–20 June — both with lower interest (55% and 56%). May was dropped: lower interest, more rain, and local-authority advice that late May is a busy period for farm machinery in the area. Around the September peak, 6–12 September showed a dip (56%) that Daniel attributed to Burning Man week; Chris said he would vote against it for that reason, so 27 September–3 October was taken as the third September week instead — Pablo: it costs nothing to include and will simply be ranked down if unpopular. Victor P asked about the June overlap with Crème Brûlée: Olivier is aware and has not suggested that event would move to July; Burning Nest has confirmed it is tied to its site permit and cannot move. Daniel added that the early dates are in any case the busiest planning season for other burns (Borderland’s grant and placement processes fall in April–May), which weighs on camp leads.
  7. Seven becomes eight. With the list at seven (31 May, 7 June, 14 June, 5 July, 13, 20, 27 September), Victor P proposed adding 6–12 September back as an eighth, to pre-empt “why did you leave this one out?” — the board can vote against it and say why. Daniel and Pablo agreed.

4. The shortlist for the asociado vote

  1. 31 May – 6 June — early June
  2. 7 – 13 June — early June (survey peak)
  3. 14 – 20 June — early June
  4. 5 – 11 July — current week (status quo)
  5. 6 – 12 September — mid-September
  6. 13 – 19 September — mid-September (survey peak)
  7. 20 – 26 September — mid-September
  8. 27 September – 3 October — mid-September
  • The current week is on the ballot as the status quo, so that members can express a preference for no change; it is not a board recommendation. Daniel said that as a board member he would be against holding the event on any of the weeks not shortlisted — the survey shows lower interest in May and October, and both shortlisted clusters plausibly address the welfare and cancellation problems of this year’s dates.
  • Every week remains subject to the INAGA (bird-nesting) permit decision, which may take months; the vote page will say that the board will adapt to circumstances and work down the list, and the voting system can recount the same ballots with any week removed.

5. Board members’ positions on each week

Basis for the positions: the board agreed it was expressing a view on viability and risk — commercial viability, cancellation and wildfire risk, permit risk — not personal preference. Several members said they would attend whichever week wins. No board-preferred week is indicated among the shortlist; the aim is to concentrate the vote on viable weeks, not to steer it.

Reasoning, week by week

31 May – 6 June

  • Victor P — against. Too close to the end of the working year for an event of this size, given the long build and strike; risk of low attendance; bird-nesting season adds permit uncertainty. (He applied the same reasoning to all the May–June weeks.)
  • Chris — against. Agreed with Victor’s reasoning.
  • Pablo V — for. No specific risk he could point to: people travel to Midburn and AfricaBurn in this period; the main issue would be conflicts with other burns (Burning Nest ends the day before), which he did not consider disqualifying.
  • Daniel T — against. Victor’s reasons, plus more rain, plus local-authority advice that late May is a heavy farm-machinery period in the area.

7 – 13 June

  • Victor P — against. Fewer conflicts with other burns than the week before, but the same bird-nesting and end-of-working-year concerns; he sees a real risk.
  • Chris — for. The most-selected week in the survey, so commercially viable; no direct clash with another event; the birds are something the association will have to deal with in any case.
  • Pablo V — for. Also the best week in the survey for build and strike availability; a good combination of every factor, with better temperatures.
  • Daniel T — for. Highest survey support; no red-alert days this year, so much better on fire risk; subject, like every week, to INAGA. This had been his own preferred week until the 3 September call moved him towards September — still a clear yes.

14 – 20 June

  • Victor P — against. As above, and edging into the more dangerous heat season — if not now, then in the projections for coming years.
  • Chris — leaning against. Decently viable commercially, but inching closer to the fire-risk period; undecided, leaning slightly against.
  • Pablo V — against. Hotter, and less of a winner in the survey than 7–13 June; if concentrating votes, this is one to leave.
  • Daniel T — against. Lower survey support and higher fire risk: had the event been on this week this year, there would have been an evacuation near the end of strike.

5 – 11 July (current week)

  • Victor P — against. Permit risk far too high and the heat has been unbearable for some years — people he knows have stopped coming for that reason alone.
  • Chris — against.
  • Pablo V — against.
  • Daniel T — against. A high probability that a permit for this week would not be granted at all; if granted, the event would very likely be suspended, and this year’s leniency from the authorities cannot be assumed to repeat. Not worth the effort only to face evacuating 3,000 people.

6 – 12 September

  • Victor P — against. A good candidate in itself, but many community members would still be on holiday or just back, and planning an event straight after that period is uncomfortable.
  • Chris — against. Proximity to Burning Man.
  • Pablo V — against. Don’t start the move with a Burning Man conflict; if the association ever wants to test that overlap, it can do so later once the impact is understood.
  • Daniel T — for, tentatively. He is less worried about the overlap: Nowhere’s community is price-conscious and only a small minority can afford Burning Man. Viable, slightly more fire risk than later September but acceptable.

13 – 19 September

  • Victor P — for. No clear risk of any kind; far enough from the holidays; high survey support.
  • Chris — for. Far enough from Burning Man; likes it.
  • Pablo V — for. Doable — people who do both events can go lighter on one — and the association would learn how much the overlap matters for future years.
  • Daniel T — for. A very clear yes, with the note that temperatures start to come down and he would personally prefer the warmer end; still fine well into September.

20 – 26 September

  • Victor P — for. Same reasons as 13–19 September.
  • Chris — for.
  • Pablo V — for. (And cooler nights mean you can wear the outfits.)
  • Daniel T — for. Commercially viable. He noted that all the September weeks appear to carry lower bird-nesting risk, so they are less likely to be moved by the permit decision.

27 September – 3 October

  • Victor P — for. His favourite: late enough that people can do their summer festivals and then come to a burn after some weeks of work; the best week for the bird permit; cooler nights and longer mornings without heat.
  • Chris — for. The only thing against it is that “October” feels wrong, which he acknowledged is not a rational argument; people who deal with it say rain is fun.
  • Pablo V — for, with reservations. It feels late, and the numbers are slightly worse than the other September weeks; but it is viable, so let members decide — it will be ranked down if they don’t want it.
  • Daniel T — for, with reservations. Personally too late; as a board member, mildly concerned that identified respondents (the more core members) are less available this week than anonymous ones. Not unviable — just less than ideal — and having voted against a week (31 May) that had more survey support, consistency argues for leaving this to the asociados.

6. Presenting the vote

  • Victor P asked for annotations on the voting page — permit risk for the birds, weather, other-event conflicts — so that the trade-offs are visible where people vote. Daniel confirmed the information pack will include a summary of the bird-nesting research (which applies to both sites) and the weather data, kept as compact and visual as possible: one summary table that carries almost everything, since many people will not click further, with detail tabs behind it. He is working on this with Céline S.
  • Pablo V proposed presenting each week with a short line on conflicts and a link to fuller reasoning; Daniel’s position is that the pack should explain the weeks on offer, not justify the selection — the selection is explained in these minutes.
  • Pablo V will try to quantify the school-holiday constraint, which he sees as the most misunderstood factor: it affected around 3.5% of survey respondents, and members who imagine it to be much larger may vote against any change on that basis alone. On the numbers, the association gains more people than it loses by moving, and the risk is lower.
  • INAGA: still no answer, and nothing yet from the bird-conservation contact. INAGA can refuse a permit on environmental grounds; June carries that risk, but so does the current week, and September is expected to be fine without being guaranteed. Chris asked what a refusal would mean in practice — nobody knows; it could be a flat no or conditions (Daniel speculated about noise or vehicle restrictions, though heavy farm vehicles already operate in the area). The ballot will state that the board will adapt to circumstances and work down the list; the voting system can recount the same ballots with any week removed.
