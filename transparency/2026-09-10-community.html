1. Event Dates — Where Things Stand

Ellen K opened on the survey-results page (shared from Daniel T’s screen once hers froze): roughly 950 answers, not bad for a 2,000-person event, and a very clear shape — a peak in the first half of June, a second peak in early-to-mid September, and the least popular dates around the current late-June/early-July slot.

Daniel T on how the shortlist was made: the sense from the community calls was for the middle option — the board pre-selects a subset of weeks that look commercially viable, and the asociados decide between them. The board settled on seven weeks plus the current one: three in June, four in September, and 5–11 July included for completeness. Board members then voted for or against each week individually — not on personal preference, but on whether an event on that week looks viable and likely to succeed — to give voters one more piece of information. Four members gave their positions on Monday’s call, three more over WhatsApp in the following days; the reasoning per week is in the board minutes. Voters may weigh the board’s view, or disregard it; the board will also vote as asociados. There are now around 120 members.

The information pack. Céline S has tidied the summary page into a single table, now in multiple languages, which will be included with the ballot as the essence of weeks of data-gathering; the sub-pages (bird nesting, temperatures, wildfire risk and so on) remain for anyone who wants the detail. Comments on how to make the table clearer are welcome until the vote is published; once it is out, it cannot be changed.

Why keep the current week but not the adjacent ones? (a question from the chat). Because the adjacent weeks are not believed to be any better: on the wildfire evidence, the weeks before and after also carried Roja Plus alerts this year — four consecutive weeks did. Moving one week either way makes no substantial difference; later weeks are worse as the fire season continues; and those weeks scored low in the survey (the week before was in the survey and drew little support, especially among anonymous respondents). The current week itself stays on the ballot because leaving it off would feel incomplete — it gives people a way to say “no, we reject everything”. The board voted unanimously against it and thinks keeping the date would be a very bad idea; the members decide.

A lesson from the survey’s wording. “Will you come?” is not “Do you like this date?” — Daniel himself answered that he would come on every week, which says nothing about which weeks he thinks are right for the event, and others answered the same way while objecting to the current dates in their comments. In hindsight the build/strike columns added little (they track attendance almost exactly) and “won’t come” was simply the mirror of “will come”, so one column of five did most of the work. Next time: two questions per date — can you come? and do you like this date?

When does the vote open? The intention was Friday 11 September; Peter D had just messaged asking whether it could go a day or two later, so: Friday with a small question mark. Daniel showed the test ballot on Humans: the information at the top, then the list of weeks, each with a drop-down — rank 1 to 8, leave unranked, or mark rejected — with the same rank allowed on several weeks. “It’s not going to be like the name vote.”

Asociado applications. If five board members vote yes on an applicant, the secretary adds them immediately. If anyone votes maybe or no, the application has to be discussed at a board meeting; everyone still in the queue is there because at least one member said maybe. The next board meeting is Monday 14 September — in time for the vote. Apologies to those waiting.

2. Questions on the Dates Process

  • Wasn’t there going to be a dedicated discussion call before the asociado vote? There was — last week’s call, announced about ten days in advance, with Condor’s and Ellen’s prepared perspectives. The questioner had expected it to come after the shortlist existed. With the vote closing on Thursday 17th there is no room for another community call before it; Discord is now the place to make a case, and anyone is free to organise a call of their own after the vote opens — Daniel will happily join and explain whatever needs explaining.
  • Joao T had missed last week (a wedding) and asked about the voting system, worried it might be as complex as the name vote or split the September vote. Daniel: the survey design was the imperfect part, not the vote. The vote is a ranked ballot, simpler than the name vote (no ranking 26 options and re-ranking them in reverse). He walked through Ranked Pairs again for those who were not there: the winning week is the one that beats every other week head-to-head; equal ranks and unranked weeks are allowed; if there is a rock–paper–scissors cycle the weakest victory is dropped; and — the counter-intuitive part — removing a week that is not winning can change the winner, which matters because INAGA may yet remove a week.
  • INAGA. No date for an answer. The bird-nesting page holds what is public (proximity to protection plans for both sites); what the agency’s reasoning is, nobody has — the original INAGA report for Nowhere, from whenever it was first authorised, has been lost, so nobody knows why an early-July week was permitted in a period that is, on the data, a concern. Requests are filed for all the dates; it is mid-summer and Spanish bureaucracy. Doing the ranked vote now means that when the answer arrives, weeks can be knocked out and the community’s choice recomputed immediately, without a second vote and another two months. People plan their year around this event — holidays, travel, which other burns — and other burns want to know too.
  • Is the result final, barring bird nesting? Yes, in practice. Daniel: this is not a vote the community has to live with for ten years regardless — if genuinely new information showed the choice would ruin the event, the community can vote again; and if INAGA refused every shortlisted week there would have to be another vote. Otherwise the result will simply be used.
  • Joao T pressed on exactly that: this decision, unlike the name or the whether-to-hold-an-event votes, will prevent some people from attending, and saying “we can always change it” leaves a door open to relitigation by whichever group is unhappy. Could it be made clear that reopening would take extreme circumstances — a legal or permit-level threat to the event — not a minor grievance? Daniel: he cannot speak for the board, whose members hold different views (as their per-week votes show), but his sense is that this board has no appetite to redo this work every month or two for frivolous reasons; some members wanted the vote later and compromised on about a month and a half; the community should have the clarity of knowing when the event is. On the wider point, his experience with Holacracy governance is that the fear of endless reopening rarely materialises: people talk, and most are mature enough to accept that nobody gets their way on everything. Many survey respondents affected by the school year wrote that they nevertheless understood why the dates have to move.
  • No date makes everyone happy. Ellen noted there is no week on which every board member agrees. Daniel: whatever is chosen — including staying put — will upset some people, justifiably; people tied to the school year are affected by every option the community favours, and that genuinely sucks. But he, and the board, sincerely believe that on the current dates the event is very likely to be cancelled or refused a permit, and a mid-event evacuation of thousands of people is worse than some people being unable to attend. Ellen: please carry the discussion on to Discord, and if you missed a call, organise one.

4. How Often Should We Hold These Calls?

Ellen opened the question: the calls have been weekly, which made sense in the run-up to the event; now the work is more like gardening. Her own instinct was once a month, becoming more frequent nearer the event.

Daniel T — first, thanks to Ellen for hosting. Having hosted nearly all of these, hosting is real mental effort: thinking ahead, finding content that is worth people’s time. More than once a month is a lot for one human, so rotate hosts rather than reduce the calls — nobody hosting more than once a month, twice at most when something big is on. And there is enough going on for weekly calls: the governance work Anna described, the dates vote, ALM preparation. The habit after an event has been to go quiet until January; a regular touchpoint — a fireplace for the people who have energy to give — keeps momentum, and 26 people on a call in September is itself a sign that people care. If nobody hosts, the call does not happen, and the problem stabilises itself. It is fine to skip a week when there is nothing to discuss.

Joao T — the community’s attention is the scarce resource. Asking people to show up at a fixed time every Thursday is a high cost; he was surprised participation had held up. If a topic is discussed the week you cannot come, it is not discussed again the week you can. His proposal: once a month, with working groups and the board continuing in between, a newsletter every week or two carrying updates (someone still has to produce it, and it has to stay meaningful or nobody reads it), and the agenda published about a week in advance so people can choose which calls they need.

Daniel T, in response: everything at a burn happens because somebody cares enough to make it happen, and asking himself why he had cared enough to run these calls for the past weeks, the answer is momentum, transparency (people can ask questions and raise topics) and a sense of togetherness — something he thinks would be lost at once a month, and the community would have a different vibe now had the weekly calls not happened. Not everyone needs to join every week; the calls should not be presented as if they do — watch the recording, read the minutes. He agreed the agenda should be set further ahead, and that planning ahead is not his strong suit.

Peter K (chat): one primary meeting plus smaller, more niche ones. Ellen: a decision-point call where everyone is wanted — the event-dates debate was one — alongside more forum-style calls. Ans (chat) offered to help with a newsletter. “This is starting to sound like a working group.”

Joao T said Daniel’s point had changed his mind: there is value in knowing that every Thursday at eight there will be familiar faces. The synthesis: keep the weekly slot whenever someone volunteers a few days ahead to host and pull together an agenda, and make the first Thursday of the month the big one — updates from all the groups, a recap of the month, anything that needs everyone — so that the person who can only attend one call attends that one, and nobody misses a decision because they could not make a given week. Ellen: more ways to reach out, a bit more structure, and spread the load that has sat on Daniel’s shoulders for six months. If you would like to host the next call, say so on Discord, show up, send an email.

1. Dates de l’événement — Où en sont les choses

Ellen K a ouvert sur la page des résultats du sondage (partagée depuis l’écran de Daniel T une fois le sien figé) : environ 950 réponses, ce qui n’est pas mal pour un événement de 2 000 personnes, et une forme très nette — un pic dans la première moitié de juin, un second pic au début-milieu septembre, et les dates les moins populaires autour du créneau actuel de fin juin/début juillet.

Daniel T sur la façon dont la présélection a été établie : le sentiment issu des appels communautaires penchait pour l’option intermédiaire — le conseil présélectionne un sous-ensemble de semaines qui semblent commercialement viables, et les asociados tranchent entre elles. Le conseil s’est arrêté sur sept semaines plus l’actuelle : trois en juin, quatre en septembre, et le 5–11 juillet inclus par souci d’exhaustivité. Les membres du conseil ont ensuite voté pour ou contre chaque semaine individuellement — non pas selon leur préférence personnelle, mais selon qu’un événement cette semaine-là semble viable et susceptible de réussir — afin de donner aux votants une information supplémentaire. Quatre membres ont donné leur position lors de l’appel de lundi, trois autres par WhatsApp les jours suivants ; le raisonnement par semaine figure dans les minutes du conseil. Les votants peuvent tenir compte de l’avis du conseil, ou l’ignorer ; le conseil votera aussi en tant qu’asociados. Il y a maintenant environ 120 membres.

Le dossier d’information. Céline S a condensé la page récapitulative en un tableau unique, désormais en plusieurs langues, qui sera inclus avec le bulletin comme l’essentiel de semaines de collecte de données ; les sous-pages (nidification des oiseaux, températures, risque d’incendie, etc.) restent disponibles pour qui veut le détail. Les commentaires sur la façon de rendre le tableau plus clair sont les bienvenus jusqu’à la publication du vote ; une fois publié, il ne peut plus être modifié.

Pourquoi garder la semaine actuelle mais pas les semaines voisines ? (une question venue du chat). Parce que les semaines voisines ne sont pas jugées meilleures : d’après les données sur le risque d’incendie, les semaines avant et après ont elles aussi connu des alertes Roja Plus cette année — quatre semaines consécutives, en fait. Décaler d’une semaine dans un sens ou l’autre ne change pas grand-chose ; les semaines plus tardives sont pires à mesure que la saison des feux avance ; et ces semaines ont obtenu un score faible dans le sondage (la semaine précédente figurait dans le sondage et a recueilli peu de soutien, en particulier parmi les répondants anonymes). La semaine actuelle elle-même reste sur le bulletin parce que la retirer donnerait une impression d’incomplétude — elle offre aux gens un moyen de dire « non, nous rejetons tout ». Le conseil a voté unanimement contre elle et pense que garder cette date serait une très mauvaise idée ; ce sont les membres qui décident.

Une leçon tirée de la formulation du sondage. « Viendrez-vous ? » n’est pas « Aimez-vous cette date ? » — Daniel lui-même a répondu qu’il viendrait chaque semaine, ce qui ne dit rien de quelles semaines lui semblent justes pour l’événement, et d’autres ont répondu de la même façon tout en contestant les dates actuelles dans leurs commentaires. Avec le recul, les colonnes construction/démontage ont peu apporté (elles suivent la participation presque exactement) et « ne viendra pas » n’était que le miroir de « viendra », si bien qu’une colonne sur cinq a fait l’essentiel du travail. La prochaine fois : deux questions par date — pouvez-vous venir ? et aimez-vous cette date ?

Quand le vote ouvre-t-il ? L’intention était le vendredi 11 septembre ; Peter D venait de demander par message si cela pouvait se décaler d’un jour ou deux, donc : vendredi avec un petit point d’interrogation. Daniel a montré le bulletin de test sur Humans : les informations en haut, puis la liste des semaines, chacune avec un menu déroulant — classer de 1 à 8, laisser non classée, ou marquer comme rejetée — le même rang étant autorisé sur plusieurs semaines. « Ça ne sera pas comme le vote sur le nom. »

Candidatures des asociados. Si cinq membres du conseil votent oui pour un candidat, le secrétariat l’ajoute immédiatement. Si quelqu’un vote peut-être ou non, la candidature doit être discutée lors d’une réunion du conseil ; tous ceux qui restent dans la file d’attente y sont parce qu’au moins un membre a dit peut-être. 4. Wie oft sollten wir diese Calls abhalten?

Ellen eröffnete die Frage: Die Calls fanden wöchentlich statt, was im Vorfeld des Events sinnvoll war; jetzt ist die Arbeit eher wie Gärtnern. Ihr eigenes Bauchgefühl war einmal im Monat, häufiger werdend, je näher das Event rückt.

Daniel T — zunächst Dank an Ellen fürs Moderieren. Da er fast alle davon moderiert hat: Moderieren ist echte mentale Arbeit — vorausdenken, Inhalte finden, die die Zeit der Menschen wert sind. Mehr als einmal im Monat ist viel für einen einzelnen Menschen, also lieber die Moderation rotieren lassen als die Calls zu reduzieren — niemand moderiert öfter als einmal im Monat, höchstens zweimal, wenn etwas Großes ansteht. Und es gibt genug los für wöchentliche Calls: die von Anna beschriebene Governance-Arbeit, die Terminabstimmung, ALM-Vorbereitung. Die Gewohnheit nach einem Event war bisher, bis Januar still zu werden; ein regelmäßiger Anlaufpunkt — ein Lagerfeuer für die Menschen, die Energie zu geben haben — hält den Schwung, und dass im September 26 Menschen in einem Call sind, ist selbst schon ein Zeichen, dass es Menschen etwas bedeutet. Wenn niemand moderiert, findet der Call nicht statt, und das Problem stabilisiert sich von selbst. Es ist in Ordnung, eine Woche auszulassen, wenn es nichts zu besprechen gibt.

Joao T — die Aufmerksamkeit der Community ist die knappe Ressource. Menschen zu bitten, jeden Donnerstag zu einer festen Zeit da zu sein, ist ein hoher Preis; er war überrascht, dass die Teilnahme sich gehalten hatte. Wird ein Thema in der Woche besprochen, in der man nicht kommen kann, wird es in der Woche, in der man kann, nicht noch einmal besprochen. Sein Vorschlag: einmal im Monat, mit Arbeitsgruppen und Vorstand, die dazwischen weiterlaufen, ein Newsletter alle ein bis zwei Wochen mit Updates (jemand muss ihn trotzdem produzieren, und er muss relevant bleiben, sonst liest ihn niemand), und die Agenda etwa eine Woche im Voraus veröffentlicht, damit Menschen wählen können, welche Calls sie brauchen.

Daniel T, als Antwort: Bei einem Burn passiert alles, weil sich jemand genug darum kümmert, es geschehen zu lassen, und fragt er sich selbst, warum er sich in den vergangenen Wochen genug gekümmert hat, um diese Calls durchzuführen, lautet die Antwort: Schwung, Transparenz (Menschen können Fragen stellen und Themen ansprechen) und ein Gefühl von Zusammenhalt — etwas, das seiner Meinung nach bei einmal im Monat verloren ginge, und die Community hätte jetzt eine andere Stimmung, hätten die wöchentlichen Calls nicht stattgefunden. Nicht alle müssen jede Woche dabei sein; die Calls sollten nicht so dargestellt werden, als müsste das sein — die Aufzeichnung ansehen, das Protokoll lesen.
