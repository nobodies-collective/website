A short call this week, with two main threads — a finance milestone and a substantial discussion of volunteer recruitment and infrastructure prioritisation for a smaller-than-usual event.

1. Toilets Paid — Finance Milestone

  • Daniel T confirmed the toilet payment has been made: €69,000 transferred today. He flagged this as an important milestone (and an eye-watering one), and hoped a future site might allow a more cost-effective approach.

2. Scale of the Event — ~2,000 vs ~3,000

  • The event is on track for roughly 2,000 tickets sold, against about 3,000 last year — around two-thirds of usual numbers. This proportionally reduces the expected volunteer pool (optimistically ~2,000 volunteers if the same sign-up rate holds), with knock-on effects on what can be built.
  • The framing throughout was that this will simply be a somewhat different event — fewer or simpler structures, with prioritisation of what genuinely must be built.

3. Volunteers and Recruitment

  • Michael M (nurse) gave the volunteer update (sharing the dashboard on screen; images to be circulated afterwards). Numbers are down markedly — and it is not only numbers but skill and experience, as some long-involved people who favoured a “no-go”/year-off earlier in the year have indeed taken the year off.
  • Strong areas: Gate, Power, Barrio Support (built up well from the ground), pre-event Nomads (nights/weekends always a struggle).
  • Greatest need: the on-site pre-event period, especially infrastructure/build labour. The final pre-event week typically needs ~44 people for infrastructure; currently ~14 are signed up. First-crew week and setup week are the thin points. Strike is, as ever, hard to staff.
  • Contingency planning is needed, but Michael M cautioned strongly against casually naming individual structures to drop — those structures have people who have planned around them year-round. Any reductions need preemptive, respectful discussion.
  • Morale and looking after volunteers remains the priority. Appreciation was expressed to all those showing up and putting in energy.
  • Daniel T added that stipends and other support are available to help people who could come but face financial barriers — please put such people in touch.

How to get in touch / who to contact

  • Discord, WhatsApp, Telegram, or any board member (Daniel, Michael/nurse, Frank, Daniela). Messages will be noticed and answered; if one person doesn’t reply, try another.

4. Recruitment Strategy — Spread the Word

  • The core recognition: existing channels (email, WhatsApp, Telegram, Discord updates) are reaching the same ~1,000–2,500 already-engaged people. The need is to reach beyond that pool.
  • The community itself is the best “comms team” — ~30 people on the call spreading the word to their own networks and barrio groups can reach thousands.
  • Comms (Nora A) will draft / re-share an easy-to-forward recruitment message within the next day or two, posted in the usual channels (Telegram, WhatsApp, Discord updates); Daniela’s link on volunteering to be shared too.
  • Personal, authentic messages (“here’s why I think you should come and build”) often land better than polished broadcasts.
  • Specific gaps to target: Facebook groups and other European burner communities (not yet really worked); the francophone community (Axel P offered to reach friends and other barrios).

5. Short-Stint and Weekend Volunteers — Do They Help?

  • In response to Tancredi T’s question about weekend/short-stay volunteers for build:
  • Yes — skilled people who come to work (not to acclimatise/party early) can make a real difference, even for a weekend. Work weekends are welcome.
  • It helps greatly to know in advance who is arriving and when, so labour can be planned and teams formed on arrival.
  • The same applies to Strike, which feels “all or nothing” but needn’t be — people are encouraged to rest in Barcelona Mon/Tue post-event and return for a couple of strike days.
  • “Build” as a term is being de-emphasised: much of the needed work is logistics, admin, and operating/decorating, not heavy lifting. There is a role for every skill set, including admin in the shade.
  • This is not a year to be picky: good attitude and willingness to be directed is enough.

6. Infrastructure Prioritisation — Decision Path

  • No decisions were made on the call — only discussion. Coordinators to convene next week to decide.
  • Daniela S will email all project coordinators to schedule a meeting Wednesday to prioritise infrastructure: map structures, estimate people-days each needs, identify where to simplify, and decide collectively what to scale back.
  • Approaches floated for simplification: build the Gate from a cabin + shade rather than a full wood build; use containers/cabins with shades on top (à la Ubertown) for communal shade/rest areas; keep things functional even where not “pretty.”
  • MoN (the large communal shade structure) was discussed as a candidate to reconsider. W, who is leading MoN, asked for clarity sooner rather than later, since teams and volunteers are already committed and a half-built outcome is the worst case. Michael M and Daniela S both affirmed their work is valued and nothing is being unilaterally cut; “maybe this is MoN’s year” was raised as a genuine open question for the smaller event (pulling people together rather than spreading thin).
  • A request was noted for an accessibility / build-schedule chart showing what is meant to be built on which day — the infrastructure team to consider producing this.

7. Appreciation and Positive Notes

  • Daniel T closed on the hard cornerstones being secured: permits, insurance, sites booked, toilets paid, and finances in good order (credit to Peter on finance/budgeting) — the event will not lose money. With those in place, ~2,000 capable, adaptable burners can still have a great week.
  • Chris O shared positive news: Malfare shift leads and Nomads are covered for Friday night — the toughest night — even if the rest of the week remains thin. Appreciation to those who signed up. More Malfare recruitment talk to come separately.

Decisions

  • No structural/infrastructure cuts decided on the call — these are explicitly discussions only until the coordinators’ meeting.
  • Coordinators to meet Wednesday to prioritise infrastructure and decide what to simplify/scale back for the smaller event.
  • Adopt a community-driven recruitment approach: a standardised, easy-to-forward message that members distribute through their own networks.

2. Größe des Events — ~2.000 gegenüber ~3.000

  • Das Event ist auf dem Weg, etwa 2.000 Tickets zu verkaufen, gegenüber rund 3.000 im Vorjahr — etwa zwei Drittel der üblichen Zahlen. Das reduziert proportional den erwarteten Pool an Freiwilligen (optimistisch ~2.000 Freiwillige, wenn die Anmelderate gleich bleibt), mit Folgewirkungen darauf, was gebaut werden kann.
  • Die durchgängige Rahmung war, dass es einfach ein etwas anderes Event wird — weniger oder einfachere Strukturen, mit Priorisierung dessen, was wirklich gebaut werden muss.

3. Freiwillige und Rekrutierung

  • Michael M (nurse) gab das Freiwilligen-Update (Dashboard auf dem Bildschirm geteilt; Bilder werden im Anschluss verteilt). Die Zahlen sind deutlich rückläufig — und es geht nicht nur um Zahlen, sondern auch um Kompetenzen und Erfahrung, da einige lang Engagierte, die früher im Jahr ein «No-go»/Sabbatjahr favorisierten, tatsächlich ein Jahr pausieren.

Die letzte Pre-Event-Woche braucht üblicherweise ~44 Personen für Infrastruktur; aktuell sind ~14 angemeldet. Die First-Crew-Woche und die Setup-Woche sind die dünnen Stellen. Strike ist, wie immer, schwer zu besetzen.
