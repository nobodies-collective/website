Sound Policy (Cocci)

Cocci summarised the group’s discussions so far and invited anyone interested to join, since sound affects a lot of people. The starting point is that complaints about sound came from everywhere on site:

  • music is still clearly audible in the green zone;
  • the RV area has no policy at all, which covers not only music but also engines and generators running to charge batteries at any time of day;
  • the current policy makes too little difference between yellow, orange and red zones: music can play loud and late in the yellow zone, and people in the green zone who expect quiet from a certain hour do not get it.

The policy needs rethinking so that it is clearer and the zones are better separated, and music does not reach the green and blue zones. Ideas being discussed:

  • Enforcement. Asking a camp to turn it down is an uncomfortable position for volunteer nomads, and nobody wants to stop a party. One idea is a dedicated sound role, perhaps with a recognisable costume, so people know who is coming to their camp and why.
  • Camps and art cars that ignore the policy. Art cars also drew many complaints. The only proposal so far, which Cocci would gladly see replaced by something better, is that a camp or art car that does not act on complaints would lose its red-zone placement or its art-car music rights the following year.
  • Build. One suggestion has been no music at all during build. Cocci would prefer a rule that music is fine as long as it does not drown out a conversation next to the speaker. For a party during build, the first step is to talk to your neighbours; where agreement cannot be reached, there need to be rules that everyone accepted before coming to the event.

Ellen: whether you like to complain about noise or about the sound policy, this is the group to join.

2. Finance Update (Peter D)

  • Reporting. With Q3 closed, the accountant needs everything that happened: about 5,000 transactions across tickets, expense reports and invoices. Could the land be bought, and how?

Paul vK asked how a purchase might be funded. Peter D: buying land is an option, not a given. If the right price does not come along, it does not happen, and the association will not take on a mortgage it cannot afford. Several structures are possible. Peter thinks the land would most likely be best held by a separate company (SL) in which the association holds an equity stake. Donations could then pass through the association, and community members could lend directly to the company. This matters because the association cannot get a conventional bank loan. People in the community have expressed interest at levels in line with what would be needed, though nothing is certain until the money arrives. The key test is whether the yearly cost of owning compares reasonably with the rent paid today; a much larger commitment might still be doable, but that is a question for the association. In Peter’s view the association would very likely have to vote on any land decision, possibly at short notice once a deal is on the table.

Pablo V added that the group is looking at ways to pay over several years rather than through a bank loan, so that payments match the potential savings. All of this depends on knowing a price, and the group will share modelling once it has one.

Who does finance?

Asked from the chat how many people are on the finance team: essentially Peter D, with Daniela handling much of the expenses work. Peter said the bottleneck right now is less the finance work itself than building the tooling in Humans. Anyone helping today would need to learn the accounting, billing, Stripe and ticketing systems, Humans and AI-assisted development faster than he could teach them. Next year, each department with a budget will have a designated person for its finances, as this year’s load was not sustainable. The 2027 Finance working group is a good place to start. Ellen: there is room for more people here as things develop. Paul vK suggested a funding team alongside the finance team, and Ellen liked the idea as a possible group.

Art project expense reports: end of October

Creativity is one of the largest and most discretionary parts of the budget. The aim is to give artists more, not less, with less paperwork. Art project expense reports must be filed with the Creativity team by the end of October (artists should already have heard from the Creativity team). After that the accounts close, because the final report for the year cannot be done while payments are still going out. Next year’s deadline will likely be the end of September, since payments in October fall into a new VAT quarter.

Paul vK warf mit einem Lächeln die Frage auf, wie die Community mit Kontakt zu Außerirdischen umgehen würde, und erklärte sich bereit, dafür einen Discord-Kanal einzurichten. Ellen erinnerte an zwei lebhafte Debatten aus früher im Jahr: ob die Veranstaltung eine Veranstaltung mit 9.000 Personen werden sollte, und ob sie während der Veranstaltung eine Offline-Community sein sollte (mit oder ohne Telefone, oder einfach mehr Gelegenheiten, das Telefon beiseitezulegen).

Auf die Frage aus dem Chat, wann Elsewhere 2027 stattfinden wird: Pablo V und Ellen bestätigten, dass die Woche vom 7.–13. Juni 2027 die von der Abstimmung der asociados gewählte ist, noch vorbehaltlich INAGA. Da es keine weiteren Themen gab, endete der Call frühzeitig.
