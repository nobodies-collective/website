Governance Meeting — Organisational Structure

Date: Monday 26 October 2026 (~64 min, video call)
Present: Céline S (facilitator), Anna T, Cupcake, Daniel T, Daniela (Production) A working session of the governance working group on organisational structure, the first after the Annual Leadership Meeting (ALM). It is not a decision-making body: the options below are drafts in preparation, to be put to the members.

The first call after the ALM. It opened with a recap of the two governance sessions there, and turned to what standing their outcomes have and how to turn them into proposals the members can decide on. Most of the call was about the one question the ALM left open: whether each department has a coordinator and deputy chosen through a process that involves people outside the department, or organises itself around an agreed list of tasks. The group agreed to draft the options as written proposals, aiming for a members’ vote at the end of November.

1. Recap of the ALM governance sessions

  • Céline S went through the summary she shared at the end of the ALM (see the roles and responsibilities and getting into and out of roles outcomes): one coordinator and one deputy per department; common reporting, tailored to each department and based on the draft event calendar Daniela presented; role descriptions for coordinators and for the board; all roles advertised; and a consent check before anyone takes up a role.
  • Still open from the ALM: selection of coordinators, deselection, and paid roles.
  • Céline S also asked who will make sure that roles are in fact always advertised. This was not settled in the call.

2. What standing the ALM outcomes have

  • Cupcake said he believed the ALM had agreed to follow a consensus decision-making process, and felt that some of the structure sessions had not followed it, so he would be wary of treating their outcomes as decisions.
  • Daniela agreed. She had recorded the outcomes as a temperature reading of the room, since several of the votes were close to evenly split, and she expects people to be waiting for a full proposal rather than taking the ALM outcomes as final.
  • Céline S noted that the working group knew this before the ALM: there is not yet a defined process for who takes which decisions, and the ALM votes were non-binding.
  • Daniel T pointed to the event-dates decision, which is widely seen as community-endorsed: engagement through community calls, the ALM and documented, minuted meetings, followed by a members’ vote. He suggested the same here: a members’ vote, not necessarily binding and not necessarily a General Assembly, as the capstone that makes the result a community decision. Cupcake agreed, adding that what made the dates vote work was the thorough discussion and information beforehand.

3. Timing

  • Daniela asked whether the proposals should go to the vote planned for December alongside the budget. Daniel T said that December will need a General Assembly for the budget and the board election, and that the election has to fall between 3 and 18 December. In his view it would be better to build a lighter, regular rhythm of members’ votes, for example a monthly set of decisions, than to put many items to a single December vote. He suggested November could work.
  • Céline S proposed working towards a sign-off at the end of November: the proposal should be prepared in advance, with input gathered through coordinators’ calls and from people with relevant knowledge, so that the vote itself is a sign-off. Anna T saw no problem with that timeline for the department proposals.
  • Anna T noted that the board role description needs a shorter timeline: with the board election coming, people who stand should know what they are and are not signing up for.
  • Daniela added that these choices are for the coming year and can be reviewed each year in the light of experience.

4. Two models for coordinating a department

  • What is agreed. It was broadly agreed that the list of tasks every department must cover is not in dispute, and that reporting should be visible to everyone, built around a shared production calendar. Every department needs a point of contact and accountability for its tasks. Daniel T proposed treating these requirements as a common baseline that sits outside both options, in the same way that every department files expenses through the same system. Anna T agreed that this is the starting point for any proposal.
  • Option A: coordinator and deputy. As set out by Anna T: each department has a coordinator and a deputy, selected through a process that involves stakeholders beyond the department (for example the board and other coordinators). They can delegate, but remain accountable for the tasks being done.
  • Option B: the department organises itself. As set out by Cupcake: clear department-level expectations (how the department invites people in, reporting, contact information), with the department free to decide how to meet them. The tasks can be split between different people, so the point of contact need not be the person making sure the tasks are covered or the person doing the reporting. There is no selection process from outside the department.
  • Daniel T summarised the difference as whether coordination is assigned through an external or an internal selection process. The tasks themselves are not in dispute.
  • Anna T’s view was that the two cannot coexist, so they should go to the community as separate proposals. Daniela added that each option is a whole package touching role descriptions, recruitment, selection and deselection, and these cannot be decided separately.

Arguments raised

  • Accountability. Daniel T said that selection inside a department is a closed feedback loop: it works while a department serves the community, but there has to be an external channel through which the community (ideally rather than the board) can redirect a department. Coordinator selection is one such channel, though perhaps not the only one.
  • Openness. Cupcake said departments must always be open: clear, published open calls and visible ways to pick up tasks, so that anyone who wants to get involved can, and has a say. The annual budget vote is the community-wide check on what departments do. Not everything a department does needs a vote. Cupcake is also working on a tool to make gaps visible and to issue open invitations to the community when help is needed. In Cupcake’s view the bigger problem today is lack of visibility, more than lack of a responsible person.
  • Where power ends up. Anna T asked what happens in each model when a department does not do what is needed, and who then picks it up. She asked whether, without coordinators, decision-making might move to a role appointed by the board (for example a paid recruitment role) or to Production. Both proposals should think this through.
  • Limits on departments. Daniela noted that departments are limited by what the community decides, the budget above all. She supported making it clear how people can join departments, and proposed organising some work as projects that people can join without committing to a whole department year-round.
  • Disagreements. Daniel T asked how a department’s direction could change under option B if people outside it disagreed. Cupcake replied that concerns would go to the department’s point of contact and its open calls, and that clear channels for this would be a big step because none exist today. Daniel T asked that the proposals set out sample scenarios for disagreements within a department, between departments, and between a department and the wider community. He noted that every option has pros and cons, and that elections are a real downside for some people.
  • Recruitment and paid roles. Anna T raised two questions that neither option yet covers: whether there should be a recruitment/HR function supporting all departments (with onboarding onto Humans, for example), and whether that would be a volunteer department or a paid role. She said these questions change where decisions are made and are for the community to decide. Céline S asked that they be factored into the proposals where possible.

5. How the proposals will be prepared

  • Céline S framed the group’s task as preparing proposals that cover the same points, so that the members are comparing like with like.
  • There were two views on how to start. One was that each participant brings their own vision next week and the group works out the options from there. The other, held by Anna T and Daniel T, was that the ALM and the survey had already produced two distinct options to be written up. Anna T said it mattered to her that the proposals are built from the community’s input (the survey and the ALM), not from the personal views of the people on the call. Daniela said that her conversations with coordinators across the year are also input that should count, beyond the survey. Daniel T agreed the input will never be complete, which is what the members’ vote is for.
  • Agreed at the close: the options are drafted as written proposals and shared in the group’s Discord, where everyone can comment on each of them. Daniela and Cupcake lead on the department-led option (B). Daniel T drafts the coordinator-and-deputy option (A), with Anna T assisting as her time allows. A third proposal is possible, but the aim is to stop at three.
  • Céline S will send the notes from this call, and the group meets again at the same time next week.

Outcomes

  • The ALM governance outcomes are treated as input and a reading of the room, not as final decisions. The open points (coordinator selection, deselection, paid roles) are to go to the members as proposals.
  • Working aim: a members’ vote at the end of November on the department model. The proposals are prepared in advance so that the vote is a sign-off.
  • Two options for how departments are coordinated: (A) a coordinator and deputy selected through a process that includes people outside the department, or (B) the department organises itself around an agreed list of tasks, with a point of contact. The common reporting requirements and task list sit outside both options, as a baseline.
  • The board role description needs to be ready sooner, ahead of the December board election.
  • The group meets weekly for now.
